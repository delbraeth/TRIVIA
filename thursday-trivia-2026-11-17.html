📜 This Day in History — November 17

  • 1558 — Elizabeth I became Queen of England when her half-sister Mary I died. Nov 17 was celebrated as "Accession Day" for more than a century.
  • 1869 — The Suez Canal opened. Empress Eugénie of France led the first convoy aboard the imperial yacht L'Aigle. Ferdinand de Lesseps built the canal, which runs about 100 miles from Port Said to Suez.
  • 1968 — The "Heidi Game." NBC switched from Jets–Raiders to the movie Heidi at 7 p.m. ET, with the Jets up 32–29 and about a minute left. Oakland scored two touchdowns in 9 seconds and won 43–32. Since then, NFL broadcasts are shown to the finish.
  • 1973 — Richard Nixon told AP editors at Disney World's Contemporary Resort, "I'm not a crook."
  • 1989 — Police beat student marchers in Prague, starting Czechoslovakia's Velvet Revolution. Within six weeks, Václav Havel was president.
  • Born today: Rock Hudson (1925), Gordon Lightfoot (1938), Martin Scorsese (1942), Lauren Hutton (1943), Danny DeVito (1944), Lorne Michaels (1944), RuPaul (1960), Jeff Buckley (1966), Sophie Marceau (1966), Rachel McAdams (1978).

🌍 Capitals

🇰🇿 What's the capital of Kazakhstan, the world's largest landlocked country? It has had four names since 1991.
Astana. It replaced Almaty as capital in 1997, was renamed Nur-Sultan in 2019, and went back to Astana in 2022.

🇲🇲 Myanmar moved its capital out of Yangon in 2005–06. What's the new purpose-built capital?
Naypyidaw (Nay Pyi Taw). It's known for nearly empty 20-lane highways.

🇲🇦 Morocco: not Casablanca, not Marrakesh. What's the capital?
Rabat

🇫🇯 What's the capital of Fiji, on the island of Viti Levu?
Suva. It's not Nadi, where the international airport is.

🎨 Arts & Literature

In 1930, who became the first American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature?
Sinclair Lewis (Main Street, Babbitt). He had turned down the Pulitzer in 1926.

In 1909, who became the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature? Her book follows a boy who rides across Sweden on a goose.
Selma Lagerlöf, The Wonderful Adventures of Nils

Which 1847 novel, published under the pen name "Ellis Bell," is about Heathcliff and Catherine Earnshaw?
Wuthering Heights, by Emily Brontë

Which Tchaikovsky ballet, which premiered in St. Petersburg in December 1892, features the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy?
The Nutcracker

Which 1939 novel, a Pulitzer winner, follows the Joad family from the Oklahoma Dust Bowl to California?
The Grapes of Wrath, by John Steinbeck

Which Beat Generation novel was typed in about three weeks in 1951 on a single scroll of paper about 120 feet long?
On the Road, by Jack Kerouac (published 1957)

🧩 General Trivia

  • GeographyKazakhstan is the world's largest landlocked country and the 9th largest overall.
  • HistoryThe oldest university still operating and granting degrees is al-Qarawiyyin in Fez, Morocco. Fatima al-Fihri founded it in 859.
  • NatureThe dodo lived only on Mauritius. The last widely accepted sighting was in 1662, less than a century after Dutch sailors arrived.
  • FoodFortune cookies come from Japanese immigrant bakers in California in the early 1900s, not from China.
  • FoodAn Italian bank, Credem, accepts wheels of Parmigiano-Reggiano as loan collateral and stores them in climate-controlled vaults. Each wheel weighs about 88 lb.
  • DrinkBourbon doesn't have to be made in Kentucky, just in the U.S. It has to be at least 51% corn and aged in new charred oak containers. About 95% of it still comes from Kentucky.
  • SpaceOn Apollo 14 in 1971, Alan Shepard hit two golf balls on the Moon using a 6-iron head attached to a tool handle.
  • HockeyThe Stanley Cup is named for Lord Stanley of Preston, Canada's Governor General. He bought the original bowl in 1892 for 10 guineas, about $50.
  • TennisWimbledon is the only Grand Slam still played on grass, and players have to wear almost all white.
  • FootballThe "Heidi Game" led NBC to install a separate phone line to its broadcast operations center, so calls about a game wouldn't jam the network switchboard again.
