📜 This Day in History — November 16

  • 1532 — In Cajamarca, Francisco Pizarro's roughly 170 Spaniards ambushed and captured the Inca emperor Atahualpa. He paid a ransom of a room filled with gold, and they executed him anyway.
  • 1776 — The "First Salute": the Dutch fort on Sint Eustatius fired a salute to the American warship Andrew Doria, the first foreign salute to the flag of the newly independent United States.
  • 1907 — Oklahoma became the 46th state, made from Oklahoma Territory and Indian Territory joined together.
  • 1959 — The Sound of Music opened on Broadway at the Lunt-Fontanne Theatre, with Mary Martin as Maria.
  • 1974 — The Arecibo message was beamed from Puerto Rico toward the globular cluster M13, about 25,000 light-years away.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceThe Arecibo message was 1,679 bits. Engineers picked that number because it's the product of two primes, 23 × 73, so a receiver would know to arrange the bits in a 23-by-73 grid to see the picture.
  • SpaceFully stacked, Starship stands about 400 feet (about 123 m) tall, making it the tallest and most powerful rocket ever flown. The catch arms on the launch tower are nicknamed "chopsticks," and the tower is called "Mechazilla."
  • HistoryThe original "Sooners" were settlers who crossed into Oklahoma's 1889 Land Run territory before the starting gun to claim the best land early.
  • HistoryAtahualpa's ransom: he offered to fill a room about 22 by 17 feet with gold once and silver twice, as high as he could reach.
  • HolidaysMexico's Revolution Day honors Nov 20, 1910, the date Francisco Madero's Plan of San Luis Potosí set for the uprising against Porfirio Díaz. The day off now falls on the third Monday of November.
  • CultureInternational Flamenco Day is Nov 16 because UNESCO added flamenco to its Intangible Cultural Heritage list on Nov 16, 2010.
  • LanguageMost Icelanders have no family surname, only a patronymic like Jónsdóttir or Jónsson ("Jón's daughter," "Jón's son"). The phone book lists people by first name.
  • WordsThe fear of buttons, for National Button Day, is called koumpounophobia.
  • TennisAndreeva is the youngest WTA Finals champion since Maria Sharapova, who won it at 17 in 2004. Andreeva's prize included a Mercedes-Benz, and she doesn't have a driver's license yet.
  • FoodThe holes in White Castle sliders were added in 1954 to help the patties steam-cook faster without flipping.
