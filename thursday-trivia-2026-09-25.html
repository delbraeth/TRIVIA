📅 This Day in History — September 25

  • 1974 — the first Tommy John surgery. Dr. Frank Jobe rebuilt the blown elbow ligament of Dodgers pitcher Tommy John using a tendon from John's own right forearm. Jobe put the odds of a return at 1 in 100. John missed a season, came back, and won 164 more games — more than he had won before the operation. Roughly a third of major league pitchers have now had it.
  • 1690 — America's first newspaper, killed after one issue. Publick Occurrences Both Forreign and Domestick was printed in Boston by Benjamin Harris. Colonial authorities shut it down four days later for publishing "without license." The next American newspaper did not appear for 14 years.
  • 1962 — Sonny Liston flattens Floyd Patterson in 2:06. At Comiskey Park, Liston took the heavyweight title with a first-round knockout. Patterson was so humiliated he left the stadium in a fake beard and dark glasses.

Also today: Congress approved twelve constitutional amendments and sent them to the states in 1789 — ten became the Bill of Rights; the Little Rock Nine attended their first full day of class in 1957 under escort by the 101st Airborne; Arnold Palmer died in 2016 at 87; and Harald Hardrada's invasion of England ended at Stamford Bridge in 1066, three weeks before Hastings.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Nauru? — It has none. (Nauru is the only country in the world with no official capital. The government sits in the district of Yaren, which is the answer most quizzes want. The whole country is eight square miles.)

What is the capital of the Netherlands — and where does the government actually sit? — Amsterdam is the constitutional capital, but Parliament, the cabinet, the Supreme Court and the monarch all work in The Hague (a split written into the constitution itself)

What is the capital of Togo? — Lomé (it sits right on the border with Ghana — you can walk from downtown into another country)

What is the capital of Indonesia? — Jakarta, but it is officially being replaced by Nusantara (a purpose-built city on Borneo; Jakarta is sinking as fast as 10 inches a year in places)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played nurse Julia Baker on Julia (1968–71)? — Diahann Carroll (the first Black woman to star in a network series in a non-servant role; she won a Golden Globe for it and had already won a Tony in 1962)

Who played the drummer — and did the "Hey hey, we're…" narration — on The Monkees (1966–68)? — Micky Dolenz (he was a child actor first, starring in Circus Boy at age 11, and could not actually play drums when he was cast; the show won the 1967 Emmy for Outstanding Comedy Series)

Who played Chico Rodriguez on Chico and the Man (1974–78)? — Freddie Prinze (he had just turned 20 when the show premiered and it was an instant top-five hit; his son Freddie Prinze Jr. was 10 months old when his father died in 1977)

Who played Michael Knight on Knight Rider (1982–86)? — David Hasselhoff (the car, KITT, was voiced by William Daniels — later Mr. Feeny on Boy Meets World — who insisted on going uncredited)

Who played retired intelligence officer Robert McCall on The Equalizer (1985–89)? — Edward Woodward (the British actor from The Wicker Man; he had a heart attack during the show's run and Robert Mitchum filled in for several episodes)

Who played Whitley Gilbert on A Different World (1987–93)? — Jasmine Guy (a trained dancer from Atlanta; Debbie Allen took over as director in season two and turned the show into a real portrait of an HBCU)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which post-Impressionist painter made the Moulin Rouge posters that essentially invented modern advertising art? — Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec (adult breaks to both thighbones left him about 4 feet 8; he died at 36)

Who was the first woman to win the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction? — Edith Wharton, in 1921, for The Age of Innocence (the jury had actually voted for Sinclair Lewis's Main Street; Columbia's trustees overruled them)

Which Russian composer wrote Pictures at an Exhibition and Night on Bald Mountain? — Modest Mussorgsky (Pictures was written for solo piano in 1874 to memorialize a dead friend's artwork; the version everybody knows is Ravel's 1922 orchestration)

Who wrote "so much depends / upon / a red wheel / barrow"? — William Carlos Williams (he was a full-time pediatrician in Rutherford, New Jersey, for over 40 years and wrote poems between patients)

Who wrote A Raisin in the Sun (1959)? — Lorraine Hansberry (the first play by a Black woman produced on Broadway; the title comes from Langston Hughes's poem "Harlem." She died of cancer at 34.)

Which novel opens, "The past is a foreign country: they do things differently there"? — The Go-Between by L.P. Hartley, 1953 (the line has long since escaped the book — it gets quoted by historians who have never read it)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Where did Pyrex come from? — Corning Glass Works, 1915 (Corning had developed heat-shock-resistant borosilicate glass for railroad lantern globes that kept shattering in the rain. Bessie Littleton, a Corning scientist's wife, baked a cake in a sawed-off battery jar to prove it would work in a kitchen.)

Who invented Vaseline? — Robert Chesebrough, patented 1872 (a chemist who noticed oil workers smearing the "rod wax" gumming up drilling rigs onto cuts and burns. He ate a spoonful of it every day and lived to 96.)

Who was Chuck Taylor? — A shoe salesman. (Converse was founded in 1908; Charles "Chuck" Taylor, a semi-pro basketball player, joined as a salesman in 1921, ran clinics out of his car for decades, and got his name on the ankle patch in 1932. He never received a royalty — just a salary and an expense account.)

Where did Red Bull come from? — Thailand, by way of Austria, 1987 (Austrian marketer Dietrich Mateschitz found that the Thai tonic Krating Daeng cured his jet lag on a business trip, partnered with its maker, carbonated it and sweetened it for Europe. "Krating daeng" literally means "red gaur," a wild bovine.)

Which hamburger chain opened the first drive-thru-only restaurant — and when? — In-N-Out, 1948 (Harry and Esther Snyder opened a 10-foot-square stand in Baldwin Park, California, with a two-way speaker box and no indoor seating and no carhops at all. Banks had drive-up windows in the 1930s; this was the first time a restaurant was built entirely around the car.)

Local: How did Cincinnati's Cintas get started? — 1929, collecting dirty rags (Richard "Doc" Farmer gathered discarded rags from Cincinnati factories, laundered them and sold them back. Three generations later it is a Fortune 500 uniform company headquartered in Mason.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceEvery planet in the solar system, lined up side by side, would just barely fit in the gap between the Earth and the Moon. The combined diameters come to about 238,000 miles; the average distance to the Moon is roughly 239,000.
  • ScienceBismuth — the pink stuff in Pepto-Bismol — was listed as the heaviest stable element for two centuries. In 2003 physicists caught it decaying. Its half-life is about a billion times the current age of the universe.
  • GeographyReno, Nevada is farther west than Los Angeles. California's coastline swings hard east below Point Conception, so San Diego sits east of Reno too.
  • AnimalsThe axolotl can regrow a limb, a jaw, parts of its spinal cord and pieces of its brain, and it never grows out of its larval form. It is also critically endangered — the wild population lives in one canal system on the edge of Mexico City.
  • BodyEach of your eyes has a blind spot where the optic nerve punches through the retina — no photoreceptors at all. You never notice it because your brain quietly paints in whatever it assumes should be there.
  • FoodVanilla orchids outside Mexico have no natural pollinator, so every pod on Earth is pollinated by hand. The technique was worked out in 1841 on Réunion by Edmond Albius, a 12-year-old enslaved boy. It is still done his way today.
  • Language"Mortgage" comes from Old French for "dead pledge" — the deal dies either when you pay it off or when you fail to.
  • HistoryThe last Civil War pension in the United States was paid out in 2020. Irene Triplett's father fought for both sides, deserting the Confederacy for the Union, and fathered her at 83. She collected $73.13 a month until she died at 90.
  • MathThe Monty Hall problem: three doors, one prize. You pick one, the host opens a losing door, and offers a switch. Switching wins two-thirds of the time. When Marilyn vos Savant published this in 1990, roughly 10,000 readers wrote in to say she was wrong — nearly 1,000 of them holding PhDs.
  • SportsIndy 500 winners drink milk because Louis Meyer asked for buttermilk in Victory Lane in 1936 — his mother's recommendation. A dairy executive saw the photograph in the paper and turned it into a tradition that has run ever since.
  • Pop CultureFitting for National One-Hit Wonder Day: "The Twist" by Chubby Checker is the only song to reach No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in two separate runs — in 1960, and again in 1962 after adults picked up the dance.
  • WeatherThe wettest inhabited place on Earth is Mawsynram in northeast India, averaging roughly 467 inches of rain a year. Villagers line the roofs of their huts with grass to muffle the noise so they can hear each other talk.
  • LocalCincinnati's Mount Airy Forest, started in 1911, was the first municipal reforestation project in the United States. The city bought out exhausted farmland and planted more than a million trees on it; at about 1,470 acres it is still the largest park in the city.
