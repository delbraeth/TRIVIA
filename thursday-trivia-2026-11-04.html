📜 This Day in History — November 4

  • 1922 — In the Valley of the Kings, Howard Carter's team found the first step leading down to Tutankhamun's tomb. On Nov 26, asked whether he could see anything, Carter answered, "Yes, wonderful things."
  • 1924 — Wyoming voters elected Nellie Tayloe Ross to replace her late husband. Sworn in on Jan 5, 1925, she was America's first woman governor, 15 days ahead of Texas's Miriam "Ma" Ferguson.
  • 1979 — Iranian students stormed the U.S. Embassy in Tehran and took 66 Americans hostage. 52 were held for 444 days. Exactly a year later, on Nov 4, 1980, Ronald Reagan was elected, and the hostages were freed minutes after he was sworn in.
  • Also: Eisenhower elected (1952). The NSA was created (1952). Soviet tanks crushed the Hungarian uprising (1956). Barack Obama elected, with a victory speech in Chicago's Grant Park (2008). Israeli PM Yitzhak Rabin assassinated (1995). Born today: Will Rogers (1879), Walter Cronkite (1916), Art Carney (1918), Doris Roberts (1925), Loretta Swit (1937), Laura Bush (1946), Robert Mapplethorpe (1946), Markie Post (1950), Kathy Griffin (1960), Ralph Macchio (1961), Matthew McConaughey (1969).

📰 Current Events

  • 🗳️ Democrats won both the House and the Senate in Tuesday's midterms. Projections had Democrats at at least 54 Senate seats, with seven flipped: Ohio, Texas, Maine, North Carolina, Iowa, Kansas and South Carolina. Ballotpedia notes that a party that won the Senate in one even year had not lost it in the next since 1954. Some House and Senate races (Alaska, Mississippi) were still uncalled.
  • 🟥 Ohio: Sherrod Brown (D) beat Sen. Jon Husted for J.D. Vance's old seat, and Amy Acton (D) beat Vivek Ramaswamy for governor. Both are flips.
  • Texas: James Talarico beat Ken Paxton, and Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott. Texas had elected only Republican governors since 1994. In Maine, Troy Jackson beat Susan Collins. Democrats also flipped governorships in Georgia (Keisha Lance Bottoms) and Iowa (Rob Sand).
  • Firsts: Hakeem Jeffries is set to be the first Black House speaker. Abdul El-Sayed (Mich.) will be the first Muslim senator, Chris Pappas (N.H.) the first openly gay man elected to the Senate, Peggy Flanagan (Minn.) the first Native American woman senator, and Annie Andrews the first woman elected senator from South Carolina.
  • On Monday, election eve, the U.S. hit Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz. An official called the strikes defensive, aimed at forces firing missiles at U.S. assets, and Iranian TV reported explosions on Qeshm Island. Qatar is mediating a possible deal: Iran would stop attacks on ships and the U.S. would lift its oil blockade.
  • The U.S. approved a sale of Patriot missiles to Ukraine. The NTSB says it recovered key parts of the Learjet that crashed into a Palm Beach County neighborhood.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Janosch, who died Nov 3 at 95, wrote a 1978 German picture book in which Little Bear and Little Tiger go looking for a land that smells of bananas. What is it called?
Oh, How Beautiful Panama (Oh, wie schön ist Panama). Nov 4 is also Panama's Flag Day.

"I never met a man I didn't like." Which humorist, born today in 1879 in the Cherokee Nation (now Oklahoma), is the line most often credited to?
Will Rogers

Which World War I poet, killed on Nov 4, 1918, a week before the Armistice, wrote a poem ending with "the old Lie: Dulce et decorum est / Pro patria mori"?
Wilfred Owen (the poem is "Dulce et Decorum Est"). His mother got the telegram as the Armistice bells were ringing.

Photographer Robert Mapplethorpe was born today in 1946. Which friend of his wrote Just Kids, the memoir of their life together that won the 2010 National Book Award?
Patti Smith

Which French composer, who died on Nov 4, 1924, wrote a Requiem with a famous "Pie Jesu" and taught Maurice Ravel?
Gabriel Fauré

Which 1996 political novel, about Gov. Jack Stanton's presidential campaign and modeled on Bill Clinton's, was published by "Anonymous"? Who turned out to be the author?
Primary Colors, by journalist Joe Klein

🏭 Founded & Invented

Four days after the Iran hostage crisis began on Nov 4, 1979, ABC launched a late-night special called The Iran Crisis—America Held Hostage. Which long-running show did it become?
Nightline (Roone Arledge's idea to compete with Johnny Carson; Ted Koppel became the anchor)

American pollster Warren Mitofsky is usually credited with inventing which election-night tool, first used for CBS in Kentucky's 1967 governor's race?
The exit poll (Dutch sociologist Marcel van Dam claims he ran one earlier that year)

Which U.S. intelligence agency was established on Nov 4, 1952, under President Truman? Its existence was so secret that people joked its initials stood for "No Such Agency."
The National Security Agency (NSA)

The Tornados' 1962 hit "Telstar," with drummer Clem Cattini (died Nov 2), was named for a communications satellite that carried the first live transatlantic TV pictures. Which company's research labs built it?
Bell Labs (AT&T)

Which UN agency's constitution took effect on Nov 4, 1946? It is headquartered in Paris and keeps the World Heritage List.
UNESCO (the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization)

🧩 General Trivia

  • PoliticsJames Talarico is the first Texas Democrat elected to the Senate since Lloyd Bentsen in 1988. That same night, Bentsen also lost the vice presidency as Michael Dukakis's running mate. Texas's "LBJ law" let him run for both offices at once.
  • PoliticsNellie Tayloe Ross never campaigned for governor. FDR later made her the first woman director of the U.S. Mint, a job she held for 20 years.
  • Words"Gerrymander" comes from Massachusetts Gov. Elbridge Gerry. In 1812 a newspaper cartoon compared one of his oddly shaped districts to a salamander.
  • EgyptOne of the daggers found with Tutankhamun has a blade made of meteoric iron, as a 2016 analysis confirmed. Today is King Tut Day.
  • HistoryWill Rogers died in a 1935 plane crash near Point Barrow, Alaska, along with the record-setting aviator Wiley Post, who was flying the plane.
  • TVWalter Cronkite ended every broadcast with "And that's the way it is." A 1972 poll named him "the most trusted man in America."
  • HockeyWinnipeg's comeback from 4–0 down started with four goals in the final 2:39 of regulation on Tuesday.
  • BaseballGold Glove voting is 75% managers and coaches and 25% statistics. Nobody can vote for a player on their own team.
  • Music"Telstar" made the Tornados the first British group to reach No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100, more than a year before the Beatles.
  • ItalyItaly's Nov 4 holiday marks the date in 1918 when its armistice with Austria-Hungary took effect, ending the war on the Italian front.
