One consolation: Cincinnati is 22–14 in one-run games and 9–4 in extras — when they keep it close, they usually win it.

Bar-bet fact: For most of the 20th century the Reds always played the first game of the entire Major League season, at home — an honor granted because Cincinnati fielded baseball's first openly all-professional team in 1869. The tradition survives as Cincinnati's Findlay Market Opening Day Parade, and Opening Day in Cincinnati is treated, unofficially, as a citywide holiday.

Tonight's fun fact: Spider-Man almost didn't happen. Stan Lee's publisher, Martin Goodman, told him readers hate spiders, teenagers could only be sidekicks, and nobody wanted a superhero with money problems. Lee buried the character in Amazing Fantasy #15 (August 1962) — a title that was already being cancelled, so nobody would notice. It became the best-selling issue Marvel had put out, and Goodman came back asking for a Spider-Man series.
