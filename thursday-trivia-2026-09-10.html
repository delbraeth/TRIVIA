📅 This Day in History — September 10

  • 1813 — Battle of Lake Erie. Oliver Hazard Perry's squadron captures an entire British fleet, then sends the note every Ohio schoolkid knows: "We have met the enemy and they are ours." It hands the U.S. control of the lake and the Northwest.
  • 1897 — London cab driver George Smith plows into a building and becomes the first person ever arrested for drunk driving. Fined 25 shillings.
  • 2008 — The Large Hadron Collider circulates its first proton beam at CERN. The world does not end; nine days later a faulty solder joint shuts it down for 14 months.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which American painter made his name pouring and flinging house paint on canvas laid on the floor? Jackson Pollock — Time nicknamed him "Jack the Dripper."

Who painted 321 covers for The Saturday Evening Post, including the "Four Freedoms"? Norman Rockwell

Which French artist, bedridden after cancer surgery, invented his late "cut-out" style with scissors and painted paper? Henri Matisse

Samuel Langhorne Clemens is better known as? Mark Twain — the pen name is riverboat slang for two fathoms, safe water.

Which Russian playwright and doctor gave us The Seagull, The Cherry Orchard, and the storytelling rule about the gun on the wall? Anton Chekhov

Who composed Appalachian Spring, Rodeo, and Fanfare for the Common Man? Aaron Copland — "Hoe-Down" from Rodeo became the "Beef. It's What's for Dinner" music.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented the cash register, and why? James Ritty, 1879 — a Dayton, Ohio saloon owner tired of bartenders skimming. He copied a ship's propeller-revolution counter and called it "Ritty's Incorruptible Cashier." The company became NCR.

Who created Gatorade, and for whom? Dr. Robert Cade's team, 1965 — for the University of Florida Gators.

Who built the first ice resurfacer? Frank Zamboni, 1949 — a Paramount, California rink owner who was really in the ice-block business until refrigerators killed it.

Who invented Life Savers, and why the hole? Clarence Crane, 1912 — a Cleveland chocolatier who wanted a candy that survived summer. The hole came from the pill-press he borrowed; his son was the poet Hart Crane.

When was the first Popsicle, and who made it? Frank Epperson, 1905 — he was 11, left soda powder and a stirring stick on the porch overnight, and patented it in 1924 as the "Epsicle."

Local: when did LaRosa's open? 1954 — Buddy LaRosa opened on Boudinot Avenue in West Price Hill after selling pizza at the Santa Maria festival.

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceGlass is not a slow-flowing liquid. Old cathedral windows are uneven because of how they were spun, not because they sagged.
  • SpaceVenus rotates backward and so slowly that its day (243 Earth days) is longer than its year (225).
  • GeographyAfrica is the only continent in all four hemispheres — north, south, east and west.
  • AnimalsA group of flamingos is a "flamboyance," and their pink comes entirely from carotenoids in their food.
  • BodyYour stomach lining replaces itself roughly every 3–4 days, or it would digest itself.
  • FoodWasabi in most U.S. restaurants is horseradish, mustard and green dye — real wasabi is one of the hardest crops in the world to grow.
  • Language"Set" has the most definitions of any English word — the OED gives it over 400.
  • HistoryOxford University is older than the Aztec Empire. Teaching there began by 1096; Tenochtitlan was founded in 1325.
  • MathIn a room of 23 people, there's a better-than-even chance two share a birthday.
  • SportsThe Olympic marathon is 26.2 miles because of the 1908 London Games — the route was stretched so it could start at Windsor Castle's nursery windows.
  • Pop CultureThe Jurassic Park velociraptor screech is mating tortoises. Sound designer Gary Rydstrom recorded them at a research facility.
  • WeatherLightning is roughly five times hotter than the surface of the sun — about 50,000°F.
  • LocalCincinnati's Roebling Suspension Bridge opened in 1866 and was the longest in the world; its designer, John A. Roebling, used it as the dry run for the Brooklyn Bridge.
  • Reds bar betThe Reds have retired #5 for Johnny Bench — and #1 for Fred Hutchinson, a manager, not a player. He died of cancer in 1964; the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in Seattle is named for him.
