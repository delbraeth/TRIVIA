📅 This Day in History — September 17

  • 1787 — The U.S. Constitution is signed at the Pennsylvania State House in Philadelphia. 39 of the 55 delegates signed; three who stayed to the end refused. Benjamin Franklin, 81 and the oldest delegate, had to be helped forward and reportedly said of the sun carved on Washington's chair that he now knew it was a rising, not a setting, sun. 239 years ago.
  • 1862 — Battle of Antietam. Roughly 22,700 men were killed, wounded or missing in a single day near Sharpsburg, Maryland — still the bloodiest day in American history. The tactical draw was enough for Lincoln to issue the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation five days later. 164 years ago.
  • 1978 — The Camp David Accords are signed at the White House by Egypt's Anwar Sadat and Israel's Menachem Begin after 13 days of secret talks brokered by Jimmy Carter. Sadat and Begin shared that year's Nobel Peace Prize; Carter got his in 2002.
  • 1972 — M*A*S*H premieres on CBS. It was nearly cancelled after a low-rated first season; eleven years later its finale drew about 106 million viewers, still the most-watched scripted episode in U.S. television history.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Eritrea?
Asmara — the whole city center is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its 1930s Italian modernist architecture, including a gas station shaped like an airplane.

2. What is the capital of North Macedonia?
Skopje — birthplace of Mother Teresa; leveled by a 1963 earthquake and rebuilt, then covered in enormous neoclassical statues in the 2010s.

3. What is the capital of Qatar?
Doha — home to roughly 80% of the country's population; the name comes from an Arabic word for a large tree.

4. What is the capital of Rwanda?
Kigali — routinely called Africa's cleanest capital; plastic bags have been banned nationwide since 2008.

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

1. Who played the black-clad gunfighter Paladin on Have Gun – Will Travel (1957–63)?
Richard Boone — a Western that opened with its hero in a San Francisco hotel wearing evening clothes and quoting poetry.

2. Who played rancher Lucas McCain on The Rifleman (1958–63)?
Chuck Connors — one of only about a dozen people to play in both the NBA (Boston Celtics) and MLB (Dodgers and Cubs).

3. Who played private investigator Joe Mannix on Mannix (1967–75)?
Mike Connors — born Krekor Ohanian; his secretary Peggy, played by Gail Fisher, was one of the first Black women in a regular dramatic role on U.S. television.

4. Who played housemother Edna Garrett on The Facts of Life (1979–88)?
Charlotte Rae — the character came over from Diff'rent Strokes, making Mrs. Garrett one of TV's rare two-series characters.

5. Who was the unseen adult voice narrating The Wonder Years (1988–93)?
Daniel Stern — grown-up Kevin. He went uncredited on screen for years and never appeared in the show.

6. Who played taxi driver Antonio Scarpacci on Wings (1990–97)?
Tony Shalhoub — hired for one episode, kept for eight seasons; later Adrian Monk.

🎨 Arts & Literature

1. Who composed The Planets (1916)?
Gustav Holst — seven movements, no Pluto: it hadn't been discovered yet. He based it on astrology, not astronomy.

2. Who painted The Hay Wain (1821)?
John Constable — ignored in England, it won a gold medal in Paris and helped set off French landscape painting.

3. "In my younger and more vulnerable years my father gave me some advice..." opens which novel?
The Great Gatsby (1925) — F. Scott Fitzgerald — it sold poorly; he died in 1940 believing he was a failure.

4. Who wrote the play Our Town (1938)?
Thornton Wilder — the only person to win Pulitzers for both fiction and drama (three total).

5. Who was the first Black author to win a Pulitzer Prize (1950, for Annie Allen)?
Gwendolyn Brooks — also wrote "We Real Cool"; later poet laureate of Illinois.

6. Which Baroque painter made the ferocious Judith Slaying Holofernes?
Artemisia Gentileschi — first woman admitted to Florence's Accademia delle Arti del Disegno.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. What was PEZ originally sold as, and where?
A peppermint breath mint for adults trying to quit smoking — Vienna, 1927. The name is a contraction of Pfefferminz. Character heads weren't added until the 1950s, for the U.S. market.

2. Who created SPAM, and what does the name mean?
Hormel, 1937 — the name won a $100 naming contest. Hormel has never officially confirmed what it stands for.

3. How was the tea bag invented?
By accident — New York tea merchant Thomas Sullivan, around 1908. He mailed samples in small silk pouches; customers dunked the whole pouch.

4. Where did Listerine get its name — and what was it first sold for?
Named for surgeon Joseph Lister; launched 1879 as a surgical antiseptic. It was also marketed as a floor cleaner and a cure for dandruff before a 1920s campaign popularized the word "halitosis."

5. Who invented the Zippo lighter, and when?
George G. Blaisdell, Bradford, Pennsylvania, 1932. The lifetime guarantee — "It works or we fix it free" — has never been changed.

6. (Local) Where was Formica invented?
Cincinnati, 1912 — by Westinghouse engineers Herbert Faber and Daniel O'Conor. It was created as an electrical insulator, a substitute for mica — hence the name. The kitchen countertop came decades later.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceOlympus Mons on Mars is about 16 miles high and 370 miles across — so wide that if you stood on it, the slope would be too gentle to notice you were on a mountain at all.
  • GeographyCanada holds roughly 62% of the world's lakes — more than the rest of the planet combined.
  • HistoryThe shortest war in history was the Anglo-Zanzibar War of August 27, 1896. It lasted about 38 minutes.
  • MathShuffle a deck of cards properly and that exact order has almost certainly never existed before. There are 52! arrangements — about 8 followed by 67 zeros.
  • FoodWorcestershire sauce was a failure. Lea and Perrins mixed a batch in 1835, hated it, and abandoned the barrel in a cellar. They tasted it again roughly 18 months later and it had fermented into something worth selling. It contains anchovies.
  • LanguageThe word "dollar" comes from Joachimsthal, a silver-mining valley in Bohemia. Its coins were called thalers.
  • SportsJames Naismith's first basketball hoops were peach baskets with the bottoms still in them. Someone had to climb a ladder and retrieve the ball after every made shot.
  • AnimalsOctopuses have three hearts and blue blood (copper-based hemocyanin). Two of the hearts stop beating when they swim, which is why they'd rather crawl.
  • BodyYou are roughly a centimeter taller in the morning — the discs in your spine compress over the course of the day and rehydrate overnight.
  • Pop CultureThat soaring eagle cry in movies and commercials is a red-tailed hawk. A real bald eagle sounds like a squeaky gull.
  • ScienceThe Mpemba effect: under some conditions hot water freezes faster than cold. It's named for Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian schoolboy who noticed it making ice cream in 1963 and was laughed at by his teacher.
  • Local (Cincinnati)Music Hall (1878) sits on a former potter's field. Human remains are still turned up during renovations — nearly 200 sets of bones were found during the 2016–17 restoration.
  • Reds bar-betErnie Lombardi is the only catcher ever to win two batting titles (.342 with the Reds in 1938, .330 in 1942) — and he was, by consensus, the slowest runner in baseball. Infielders played him on the outfield grass.
