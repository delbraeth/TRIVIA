📅 This Day in History — September 14

  • 1752 — Britain and its colonies finish switching to the Gregorian calendar: Sept 2 was followed directly by Sept 14. Eleven days simply never happened.
  • 1814 — After watching the night-long British bombardment of Fort McHenry, Francis Scott Key writes the poem that becomes "The Star-Spangled Banner" (made the national anthem in 1931).
  • 1901 — President William McKinley, of Niles, Ohio, dies in Buffalo eight days after being shot. Theodore Roosevelt, 42, becomes the youngest president in US history.
  • 1959 — The Soviet probe Luna 2 crashes into the Moon, the first human-made object to reach another world.
  • 1960 — OPEC is founded in Baghdad by Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and Venezuela.

🗺️ Capitals Quiz

Capital of Ecuador? — Quito (at 9,350 ft, the highest official capital in the world; in 1978 it became one of the very first UNESCO World Heritage Sites)

Capital of Vietnam? — Hanoi (the name means "inside the river"; the city celebrated its 1,000th birthday in 2010 — Ho Chi Minh City is larger but not the capital)

Capital of Iceland? — Reykjavík ("smoky bay," for the steam from its hot springs; the northernmost capital of a sovereign state)

Capital of Botswana? — Gaborone (purpose-built at independence in 1966 and named for Chief Gaborone; one of the fastest-growing cities in the world for decades)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who built the first Ferris wheel, and why? — George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., 1893 — commissioned for the Chicago World's Fair to give America an answer to the Eiffel Tower.

Who invented the modern trampoline? — George Nissen, 1936 — a gymnast inspired by trapeze safety nets; he took the name from the Spanish trampolín.

What was the first home video game console? — The Magnavox Odyssey, 1972 — designed by Ralph Baer; it had no sound and used plastic screen overlays for color.

Who invented cotton candy? — A dentist — William Morrison and confectioner John C. Wharton patented the machine in 1897 and sold it at the 1904 World's Fair as "Fairy Floss."

Who invented the aircraft "black box" flight recorder? — David Warren, Australia, 1950s — and it isn't black; it's painted bright orange.

🏙️ When were the Cincinnati Red Stockings founded? — 1869 — the first openly all-salaried professional baseball team, which went undefeated on a coast-to-coast barnstorming tour.

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceBananas are slightly radioactive — they're rich in potassium, and a fraction of all potassium is the isotope potassium-40.
  • SpaceA single day on Mercury (sunrise to sunrise) lasts about 176 Earth days — twice as long as its 88-day year.
  • GeographyRussia spans 11 time zones, but France has the most of any country — 12, thanks to its overseas territories.
  • AnimalsWombats produce cube-shaped droppings — the shape keeps them from rolling off the rocks they use as scent markers.
  • BodyThe cornea is the only part of the body with no blood supply; it takes oxygen directly from the air.
  • FoodCarrots were originally purple, white and yellow. Orange carrots were cultivated in the 17th-century Netherlands.
  • LanguageThe dot over a lowercase i or j has a name: a tittle.
  • HistoryWyoming was the first US territory to grant women the vote — in 1869, 51 years before the 19th Amendment.
  • MathA "jiffy" is a real unit: in electronics it's one hundredth of a second; in physics it's the time light takes to cross a fermi.
  • SportsBasketball's 24-second shot clock was invented in 1954 by Syracuse Nationals owner Danny Biasone, who divided 2,880 seconds of game time by the 120 shots a good game averaged.
  • Pop CultureThe "Wilhelm scream" — first recorded for a 1951 Western — has been dropped into more than 400 films and shows as an inside joke.
  • WeatherThe strongest surface wind gust ever recorded was 253 mph, on Barrow Island, Australia, during Cyclone Olivia in 1996.
  • LocalCincinnati's Over-the-Rhine holds one of the largest surviving collections of Italianate architecture in the United States.
  • Reds bar-betThe Reds' only perfect game was Tom Browning's on Sept 16, 1988 — first pitch came at 9:59 p.m. after a two-and-a-half-hour rain delay.
