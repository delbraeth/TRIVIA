📜 This Day in History — October 13

  • 1775 — The Continental Congress orders two armed ships fitted out, the founding act of the U.S. Navy. Today is its 251st birthday.
  • 1792 — The cornerstone of the White House is laid. Irish-born architect James Hoban designed it; John Adams was its first resident, in 1800.
  • 1960 — Bill Mazeroski homers in the bottom of the 9th at Forbes Field: Pirates 10, Yankees 9 in Game 7. It's still the only walk-off home run to end a World Series Game 7.
  • Also: Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571, carrying a rugby team, crashes in the Andes (1972); 16 survived 72 days. Born today: Margaret Thatcher (1925), Paul Simon (1941), Jerry Rice (1962).

🌍 World Capitals

🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia — the largest country in the world with no permanent river. Capital?
Riyadh. The name means "the gardens."

🇨🇮 Côte d'Ivoire — its biggest city is Abidjan, but that's not the capital. What is?
Yamoussoukro, capital since 1983. Its Basilica of Our Lady of Peace was modeled on St. Peter's in Rome.

🇲🇪 Montenegro — its capital was called Titograd from 1946 to 1992. What's it called now?
Podgorica

🇫🇲 Federated States of Micronesia — a Pacific nation of 600+ islands. Capital?
Palikir, on Pohnpei, near the ancient stone city of Nan Madol.

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Happy 251st, U.S. Navy: who played Navy lawyer Harmon "Harm" Rabb Jr. on JAG (1995–2005)?
David James Elliott. JAG launched NCIS with a two-part backdoor pilot in 2003.

Who wore the coonskin cap as frontiersman Daniel Boone on NBC (1964–70)?
Fess Parker, who had already worn one as Disney's Davy Crockett

On Highway to Heaven (1984–89), Michael Landon played the angel Jonathan Smith. Who played his human partner, ex-cop Mark Gordon?
Victor French, Landon's co-star from Little House on the Prairie

Which Rolling Stones song was the theme for the Vietnam War drama Tour of Duty (1987–90)?
"Paint It Black." Terence Knox starred as Sgt. Zeke Anderson.

On Riptide (1984–86), the detectives lived on a boat in King Harbor, Calif. Who played Cody Allen?
Perry King (Joe Penny played Nick Ryder; Thom Bray was "Boz," who built the robot Roboz)

Which star played ex-Steelers QB turned small-town Arkansas coach Wood Newton on Evening Shade (1990–94)?
Burt Reynolds. He won the 1991 Emmy for lead actor in a comedy.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which Herman Wouk novel, which won the 1952 Pulitzer, features Captain Queeg and his missing strawberries?
The Caine Mutiny. Humphrey Bogart played Queeg in the 1954 film.

Tom Clancy's first novel (1984) was the first fiction ever published by the Naval Institute Press. Name it.
The Hunt for Red October

Which Russian composer, a former naval officer, wrote Scheherazade and "Flight of the Bumblebee"?
Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov

In 1913, who became the first non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature? (Hard)
Rabindranath Tagore (Gitanjali). He wrote the national anthems of both India and Bangladesh.

Which Russian poet wrote the verse novel Eugene Onegin and died after a duel in 1837?
Alexander Pushkin

Which Italian writer, born Oct 15, 1923, wrote If on a winter's night a traveler, a novel told partly in the second person?
Italo Calvino (born in Cuba)

🏭 Founded & Invented

David Bushnell built the first submarine used in combat in 1775. What was it called?
The Turtle. In 1776 it tried, and failed, to sink HMS Eagle in New York Harbor.

The Rueckheim brothers of Chicago trademarked which snack in 1896? Its mascot is a sailor.
Cracker Jack. Sailor Jack and his dog Bingo arrived in 1918.

Al Copeland opened "Chicken on the Run" near New Orleans in 1972. What did he rename it?
Popeyes, after Popeye Doyle in The French Connection

Which company launched the Walkman portable cassette player in 1979?
Sony

Mary Phelps Jacob patented the first modern version of what in 1914, made from two handkerchiefs and ribbon?
The brassiere. She sold the patent to Warner Brothers Corset Company for $1,500.

Sudoku was invented by Indiana architect Howard Garns in 1979. What was it originally called?
"Number Place." The Japanese publisher Nikoli named it Sudoku in 1984.

🧠 General Trivia

  • Navy"Anchors Aweigh" was written in 1906 by Lt. Charles Zimmermann, the Naval Academy bandmaster, for the Army–Navy game.
  • HistoryThe White House was officially called the "President's House" or "Executive Mansion" until Theodore Roosevelt made "White House" official in 1901.
  • GeographyOn Oct 13, 1884, the International Meridian Conference chose Greenwich as the prime meridian. France abstained and kept Paris time until 1911.
  • FoodBritain's Royal Society of Chemistry ruled in 2008 that a Yorkshire pudding must be at least 4 inches tall to count.
  • NFLPuka Nacua caught 17 passes Monday night. The single-game record is 21, set by Brandon Marshall of the Broncos in 2009.
  • NFLJerry Rice, born today in 1962, holds the career records for receiving yards (22,895) and receiving TDs (197).
  • MLBCleveland last won the World Series in 1948, the longest active title drought in MLB. The Rays have never won one (lost in 2008 and 2020).
  • MLBThe Brewers' only World Series trip came in 1982, when they were an American League team. They moved to the NL in 1998.
  • HistoryOn Friday, Oct 13, 1307, King Philip IV of France had the Knights Templar arrested across his kingdom. It's often cited, on thin evidence, as a source of the Friday-the-13th superstition.
  • PeopleMargaret Thatcher, born today in 1925, worked as a research chemist after studying chemistry at Oxford, before turning to law and politics.
