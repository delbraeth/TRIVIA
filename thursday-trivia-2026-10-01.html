📅 This Day in History — October 1

  • 1971 — Walt Disney World opens near Orlando, with only the Magic Kingdom. Walt had died in 1966, and his brother Roy insisted on putting "Walt" in the name. Disney had bought about 27,000 acres through dummy companies so land prices wouldn't spike. Adult admission was $3.50. 55 years ago today.
  • 1961 — Roger Maris hits No. 61 off Boston's Tracy Stallard in the final game of the season, breaking Babe Ruth's record of 60. Only 23,154 fans were at Yankee Stadium. Commissioner Ford Frick ruled the record would be listed separately because the season was 162 games instead of 154. The famous "asterisk" never actually appeared in the record books.
  • 1908 — Ford introduces the Model T at $850. Moving-assembly-line production pushed the price under $300 by the mid-1920s, and more than 15 million were built.

Also today: Babe Ruth's "called shot" at Wrigley Field in the 1932 World Series; Jimmy Carter born in Plains, Georgia, in 1924 (he later became the first president to reach 100); Johnny Carson's first Tonight Show in 1962, introduced by Groucho Marx; EPCOT opened in 1982; and Nigeria (1960) and Cyprus (1960) mark their independence days.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Djibouti? — Djibouti (City) (the country and its capital share a name. Djibouti hosts Camp Lemonnier, the only permanent U.S. military base in Africa, and in 2017 it became home to China's first overseas military base. Lake Assal, about 500 ft below sea level, is the lowest point in Africa.)

What is the capital of Vanuatu? — Port Vila (until 1980 the islands were the New Hebrides, ruled jointly by Britain and France as a "condominium." That meant two police forces, two legal systems and two school systems.)

What is the capital of Liechtenstein? — Vaduz (Liechtenstein is one of only two doubly landlocked countries, along with Uzbekistan. It is also a major exporter of false teeth.)

What is the capital of Cabo Verde (Cape Verde)? — Praia (it is on Santiago island, off West Africa. Cabo Verde's best-known export is singer Cesária Évora, the "Barefoot Diva," who performed without shoes.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Ann Marie, the aspiring actress, on That Girl (1966–71)? — Marlo Thomas (one of the first sitcoms about a single working woman who didn't live with her parents. Her father was Danny Thomas, who founded St. Jude Children's Research Hospital.)

Who played Kookie, the hair-combing parking-lot attendant on 77 Sunset Strip? — Edd Byrnes (the character was popular enough to get a novelty single, "Kookie, Kookie (Lend Me Your Comb)," with Connie Stevens. It reached No. 4 in 1959.)

Who played Diana Prince on Wonder Woman (1975–79)? — Lynda Carter (she had been Miss World America 1972. The spin she used to transform was her own idea.)

Who played Patty Greene on the cult sitcom Square Pegs (1982–83)? — Sarah Jessica Parker (it lasted one season. The theme song was by new-wave band The Waitresses, and the show was created by original SNL writer Anne Beatts.)

Who played Erica Kane on All My Children? — Susan Lucci (she was nominated for the Daytime Emmy 18 times without winning and finally won in 1999, on her 19th nomination.)

Who played the title role on Xena: Warrior Princess (1995–2001)? — Lucy Lawless (Xena was a spinoff of Hercules: The Legendary Journeys, where Lawless had already played other characters. The series was filmed in New Zealand.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which novel begins "Scarlett O'Hara was not beautiful, but men seldom realized it…"? — Gone with the Wind — Margaret Mitchell (1936) (it won the 1937 Pulitzer. In Mitchell's drafts, the heroine's name was Pansy.)

Which Pittsburgh-born painter was the only American to exhibit with the French Impressionists? — Mary Cassatt (Edgar Degas invited her to join the group. She is best known for intimate paintings of mothers and children.)

Which composer wrote the march played at nearly every American graduation? — Edward Elgar — Pomp and Circumstance March No. 1 (the U.S. tradition started at Yale in 1905, when it was played as Elgar received an honorary doctorate.)

Who was the first American woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature? — Pearl S. Buck (1938) (she grew up in China, and her novel The Good Earth won the 1932 Pulitzer.)

Who wrote Dracula (1897), and what was his day job? — Bram Stoker — business manager of London's Lyceum Theatre (he worked for the star actor Henry Irving, who is often named as a model for the Count.)

Which poet wrote "Auld Lang Syne"? — Robert Burns (1788) (Scotland celebrates him with Burns Night every Jan. 25, with haggis and a recitation of his "Address to a Haggis.")

🏭 Founded & Invented

What was Pepsi originally called? — "Brad's Drink" (1893) (pharmacist Caleb Bradham created it in New Bern, North Carolina, and renamed it Pepsi-Cola in 1898. The name is usually traced to dyspepsia, or indigestion.)

What did Ferruccio Lamborghini build before sports cars? — Tractors (the story goes that he complained to Enzo Ferrari about the clutch in his Ferrari, was told to stick to tractors, and started his own car company in 1963.)

What were Q-tips first called? — "Baby Gays" (1923) (Leo Gerstenzang came up with them after watching his wife wrap cotton around toothpicks to clean their baby's ears. The "Q" stood for quality.)

What TV moment made Twister a hit in 1966? — Johnny Carson playing it with Eva Gabor on The Tonight Show (Milton Bradley's game had been accused of being "sex in a box." After the broadcast, sales took off.)

What did Samsung sell when it was founded in 1938? — Dried fish, vegetables and noodles (Lee Byung-chul started it as a trading company in Daegu, Korea. "Samsung" means "three stars." It didn't make electronics until 1969.)

LocalWhere did the first United Dairy Farmers store open? — Norwood, Ohio — May 8, 1940 (Carl Lindner Sr. and his children wanted to sell milk directly and cut out the delivery middleman. His son Carl Lindner Jr. later became principal owner of the Reds.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • FoodThe Pumpkin Spice Latte had no pumpkin in it for 12 years. Starbucks launched it in 2003 and didn't add real pumpkin until 2015. Today is National Pumpkin Spice Day.
  • DrinkCoffee's origin legend involves dancing goats. An Ethiopian goatherd named Kaldi supposedly noticed his goats getting lively after eating red coffee cherries. It's International Coffee Day.
  • MythYou can't see the Great Wall of China from the Moon. It's long but narrow, about the color of the ground around it. China's first astronaut, Yang Liwei, said in 2003 that he couldn't see it even from low orbit.
  • HistoryThe Leaning Tower of Pisa started leaning during construction, in the 1170s, because of soft soil. It took about 200 years to finish, and engineers straightened it by about 17 inches between 1990 and 2001.
  • EngineeringThe Golden Gate Bridge's color is "International Orange." It started as the primer, and the architect kept it. The Navy had wanted the bridge painted in black and yellow stripes.
  • SportsVolleyball was originally called "Mintonette." William G. Morgan invented it in 1895 in Holyoke, Massachusetts, four years after basketball was invented in nearby Springfield.
  • SportsAbner Doubleday did not invent baseball. A 1907 commission gave him the credit, but in 1839, when he was supposed to have invented it in Cooperstown, he was a cadet at West Point.
  • HistoryGutzon Borglum didn't live to finish Mount Rushmore. He died in March 1941, and his son, Lincoln Borglum, completed the work that October.
  • HoaxThe famous 1934 Loch Ness Monster photo was fake. The "monster" was a model head attached to a toy submarine, and one of the people involved confessed in 1994.
  • LocalGoetta is a Cincinnati specialty: a German-style breakfast sausage of pork, beef and steel-cut "pinhead" oats, sliced and fried crisp. Glier's in Covington is the largest producer, and the region holds an annual Goettafest.
