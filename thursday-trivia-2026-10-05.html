📅 This Day in History — October 5

  • 1962 — The Bond era and the Beatles era start on the same day. Dr. No, the first James Bond film, premiered in London, and the Beatles released their first single, "Love Me Do," in the U.K. 64 years ago today.
  • 1969 — Monty Python's Flying Circus premieres on BBC One. Its working titles included "Owl Stretching Time" and "A Horse, a Spoon and a Basin."
  • 1877 — Chief Joseph surrenders in Montana's Bear Paw Mountains, about 40 miles short of the Canadian border, after leading the Nez Perce on a flight of roughly 1,170 miles. His surrender speech ends: "From where the sun now stands, I will fight no more forever."

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Tonga? — Nukuʻalofa (on Tongatapu. Tonga is the last Polynesian kingdom and was never formally colonized. Captain Cook called them the "Friendly Islands.")

What is the capital of Brazil? — Brasília (not Rio or São Paulo. It was built from scratch in about four years and became the capital in 1960. Seen from above, Lúcio Costa's city plan looks like an airplane or a bird. Oscar Niemeyer designed its major buildings.)

What is the capital of Burundi? — Gitega (it became the political capital in 2019. Bujumbura, on Lake Tanganyika, is still the largest city and economic center, and the answer older quiz books give.)

What is the capital of Saint Lucia? — Castries (Saint Lucia is the only country named after a woman, Saint Lucy. With about 180,000 people, it has produced two Nobel laureates: economist Sir Arthur Lewis (1979) and poet Derek Walcott (1992). Both were born on January 23.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Pvt. Gomer Pyle, the Marine who said "Shazam!" and "Golly!", on Gomer Pyle, U.S.M.C. (1964–69)? — Jim Nabors (the show was a spinoff of The Andy Griffith Show. Nabors had a booming operatic baritone and sang "Back Home Again in Indiana" before the Indy 500 for decades.)

Who played "Uncle Martin," the stranded alien with retractable antennae, on My Favorite Martian (1963–66)? — Ray Walston (later he played the strict teacher Mr. Hand in Fast Times at Ridgemont High, and he won an Emmy as Judge Bone on Picket Fences.)

Who played the intimidating Harvard law professor Charles W. Kingsfield on The Paper Chase? — John Houseman (he won an Oscar for the same role in the 1973 film. He was also the Smith Barney pitchman: "They make money the old-fashioned way. They earn it.")

Who played Thelma "Mama" Harper on Mama's Family (1983–90)? — Vicki Lawrence (the character came from sketches on The Carol Burnett Show. Lawrence was in her 30s, playing a woman in her 70s, and she had a 1973 No. 1 hit, "The Night the Lights Went Out in Georgia.")

Who played the stuttering, wisecracking "computer-generated" host on Max Headroom (ABC, 1987)? — Matt Frewer (Max wasn't really a computer graphic. It was Frewer in prosthetic makeup in front of a hand-drawn background. Max also starred in New Coke ads: "Catch the wave!")

Who played the son of Zeus on the syndicated hit Hercules: The Legendary Journeys (1995–99)? — Kevin Sorbo (it was filmed in New Zealand, and its spinoff, Xena: Warrior Princess, eventually drew bigger ratings.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which French philosopher, born on this day in 1713, spent about 25 years editing the Encyclopédie? — Denis Diderot (the "Diderot effect" comes from his essay about a gift of an elegant new robe. Next to it his old belongings looked shabby, so he ended up replacing nearly everything.)

Which spy novelist also wrote the children's book Chitty-Chitty-Bang-Bang? — Ian Fleming (it came out in 1964, the year he died. The first Bond film, Dr. No, premiered on this day in 1962.)

Hazel, Fiver and Bigwig are rabbits in which 1972 novel? — Watership Down — Richard Adams (it started as stories Adams told his daughters on long car rides. Several publishers rejected it before it became a classic.)

Which English painter created The Blue Boy (c. 1770)? — Thomas Gainsborough (in 1921 Henry Huntington bought it for a record $728,000 and took it to California. In 2022 it went back to London's National Gallery on loan, 100 years after it left.)

"Listen, my children, and you shall hear / Of the midnight ride of ___." Name the hero and the poet. — Paul Revere — Henry Wadsworth Longfellow (Longfellow wrote it in 1860, 85 years after the ride, and got a lot wrong. Revere never reached Concord, and William Dawes and Samuel Prescott rode that night too.)

Who composed "The Stars and Stripes Forever," the official National March of the United States? — John Philip Sousa (known as "the March King," he led the U.S. Marine Band from 1880 to 1892. The sousaphone is named after him.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Which famous sandwich cookie did Nabisco introduce in 1912, four years after its rival Hydrox? — The Oreo (it was first made at Nabisco's bakery in Manhattan's Chelsea neighborhood, now Chelsea Market. Hydrox came first, but many people now assume it was the knockoff.)

What was Kleenex originally sold for when Kimberly-Clark introduced it in 1924? — Removing makeup and cold cream (the material, cellucotton, had been developed for WWI bandages and gas-mask filters. Customers kept writing in to say they used the tissues to blow their noses, so Kimberly-Clark started marketing them that way.)

OhioIn which city did Vincent Marotta and Samuel Glazer launch the Mr. Coffee drip maker in 1972? — Cleveland, Ohio (its pitchman was Joe DiMaggio, and it sold so well that most American kitchens soon switched from percolators to drip machines.)

Ranch dressing is named after which real California dude ranch? — Hidden Valley Ranch (Steve and Gayle Henson ran it near Santa Barbara, where Steve served his buttermilk dressing to guests. He sold the brand to Clorox in 1972 for $8 million.)

Irving Naxon invented a slow-cooking bean pot. What did Rival rename it in 1971? — The Crock-Pot (Naxon was inspired by his grandmother in Lithuania, who left her Sabbath stew, cholent, in the town bakery's cooling oven overnight.)

What was the first video ever uploaded to YouTube (April 23, 2005)? — "Me at the zoo" (it's 19 seconds of co-founder Jawed Karim at the San Diego Zoo elephants. YouTube was founded that year by Karim, Chad Hurley and Steve Chen, and Google bought it in 2006 for $1.65 billion.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • TechThe Python programming language is named after Monty Python, not the snake. Guido van Rossum was reading the show's scripts when he named it. Flying Circus premiered on this day in 1969.
  • GeographyThe Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench is about 36,000 feet deep. If you dropped Mount Everest in, its peak would still be more than a mile underwater.
  • GeographyMonaco is smaller than New York's Central Park: about 0.8 square miles against 1.3.
  • GeographyMount Kilimanjaro (19,341 ft) is the tallest free-standing mountain on Earth. It stands alone rather than as part of a range.
  • HistoryAlbert Einstein was offered the presidency of Israel in 1952. He turned it down, saying he lacked the "natural aptitude and the experience to deal properly with people."
  • FoodPeach Melba was created by chef Auguste Escoffier at London's Savoy for the Australian opera star Nellie Melba. So was Melba toast.
  • FoodGranny Smith apples are named after a real grandmother, Maria Ann Smith, who found the chance seedling near Sydney, Australia, around 1868. Today is National Apple Betty Day.
  • HistoryRhode Island was first to declare independence from Britain (May 4, 1776) and last of the 13 to ratify the Constitution (May 29, 1790). Today is National Rhode Island Day.
  • LocalThe Freezer Bowl: the Bengals beat San Diego 27–7 in the January 1982 AFC Championship at Riverfront. It was −9°F with a wind chill of −59°F, the coldest game in NFL history by wind chill.
  • AnimalsThe "three-second goldfish memory" is a myth. In studies, goldfish remembered tasks for months and learned to push levers for food.
