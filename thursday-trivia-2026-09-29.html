📅 This Day in History — September 29

  • 1954 — "The Catch." Game 1 of the World Series at the Polo Grounds, score tied 2–2 in the eighth, two on. Vic Wertz hits it about 420 feet to dead center and Willie Mays runs it down over his shoulder with his back to the plate — then spins and fires the ball back in before the runners can advance. The Giants win in ten and sweep Cleveland. Mays always said the throw was the harder play. 72 years ago today.
  • 1982 — The Tylenol murders begin. Seven people in the Chicago area died after taking Extra-Strength Tylenol capsules laced with cyanide; the first three deaths came on this day. The case was never solved, but it rewrote American consumer packaging: tamper-evident seals and the shift from capsules to solid caplets both come directly from it.
  • 1913 — Rudolf Diesel vanishes. The inventor of the diesel engine boarded a steamer from Antwerp to Harwich, left his hat and folded overcoat on the deck rail, and was never seen alive again. A body was recovered ten days later and identified by his son from items in the pockets. Theories have run from suicide to German naval sabotage ever since.

Also today: British forces breached the Hindenburg Line in 1918 after a 56-hour bombardment, effectively ending Germany's defensive war; the Babi Yar massacre outside Kyiv began in 1941; Stacy Allison became the first American woman to summit Everest in 1988; the Dow fell a then-record 777.68 points in 2008 after the first bailout vote failed; and Gene Autry was born in Tioga, Texas, in 1907.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Palau? — Ngerulmud (the least-populated national capital on Earth — a purpose-built government complex opened in 2006 with a few hundred residents. Almost everyone still lives in the old capital, Koror.)

What is the capital of Benin? — Porto-Novo (a classic pub-quiz trap: Porto-Novo is the official capital and seat of the legislature, but the president, the ministries and the embassies are all in the much larger Cotonou.)

What is the capital of Moldova? — Chișinău (pronounced roughly kish-i-NOW. Moldova sits between Romania and Ukraine, and its wine cellars at Mileștii Mici hold the largest wine collection in the world — about two million bottles.)

What is the capital of the Bahamas? — Nassau (on New Providence island, and named for William III of Orange-Nassau. In the early 1700s it was effectively run by pirates — Blackbeard among them — until the Crown sent a governor to clear them out.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Allison MacKenzie on Peyton Place (1964–69)? — Mia Farrow (America's first successful prime-time soap, airing two and three nights a week. It made Farrow and Ryan O'Neal stars; she left in 1966 and cut her hair off on the way out.)

Who played Commander John Koenig on Space: 1999 (1975–77)? — Martin Landau (the premise: a nuclear waste dump on the far side of the Moon explodes and blasts the Moon out of Earth orbit. It was the most expensive British series ever made at the time, from Gerry and Sylvia Anderson.)

Who played Mindy McConnell on Mork & Mindy (1978–82)? — Pam Dawber (the show spun out of a single dream-sequence episode of Happy Days. Scripts were written with deliberate blank stretches marked "Mork can do his thing" so Robin Williams could improvise.)

Who played Sgt. T.J. Hooker on T.J. Hooker (1982–86)? — William Shatner (a veteran cop who turned down promotion to stay on patrol. Heather Locklear and Adrian Zmed rode along; Shatner insisted on doing his own hood-sliding.)

Who played Mary Jenkins on 227 (1985–90)? — Marla Gibbs (adapted from a stage play Gibbs had performed in. Jackée Harry, as the neighbor Sandra, became the first Black actress to win the Emmy for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy.)

Who played the teenage physician on Doogie Howser, M.D. (1989–93)? — Neil Patrick Harris (created by Steven Bochco and David E. Kelley. Every episode closed the same way — Doogie typing a few lines of diary into his computer in blue text.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which poet wrote "I wandered lonely as a cloud / That floats on high o'er vales and hills"? — William Wordsworth ("Daffodils," 1807. The walk that inspired it was his sister Dorothy's — her journal entry describing the flowers came first, and he borrowed from it.)

Who painted The Swing (1767) — the pink dress, the lost slipper, the man hidden in the bushes? — Jean-Honoré Fragonard (the definitive Rococo painting, and a scandalous commission: the patron asked for a bishop to push the swing. Fragonard painted an unwitting husband instead.)

Who composed Carmina Burana, which opens with "O Fortuna"? — Carl Orff (1936, set to bawdy medieval poems found in a Bavarian monastery. Orff told his publisher to destroy everything he had written before it.)

Who wrote The Wind in the Willows (1908)? — Kenneth Grahame (it grew out of bedtime stories for his son Alastair. Grahame was Secretary of the Bank of England at the time.)

Who wrote The Master and Margarita? — Mikhail Bulgakov (the devil arrives in Soviet Moscow with a giant talking cat. Bulgakov burned an early draft, finished it in secret, and died in 1940 — it was not published until the 1960s, censored.)

Which American novelist won Pulitzers for Rabbit Is Rich and Rabbit at Rest? — John Updike (four novels about Harry "Rabbit" Angstrom, written roughly one per decade from 1960 to 1990 — a running portrait of middle-class America.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented ibuprofen, and how did he first test it? — Stewart Adams (with John Nicholson at Boots in Nottingham; patented 1961. Adams took the first human dose himself — on a hangover, before giving a speech in Moscow. It worked.)

Who made the elevator safe, and how did he prove it? — Elisha Otis (his 1852 safety brake stopped a falling car. At the 1854 New York Crystal Palace he stood on a raised platform and had an assistant cut the rope. It dropped a few inches and held. Skyscrapers follow from that stunt.)

Who invented the machine that makes flat-bottomed paper grocery bags? — Margaret Knight (patented 1871. A man who had seen her machine being built filed first, arguing a woman could not have designed it. She produced her drawings and notes in court and won.)

Why is there a rooster on the Huy Fong sriracha bottle? — It's founder David Tran's zodiac sign (born in the Year of the Rooster, 1945. He started the company in Los Angeles in 1980 after leaving Vietnam, and famously never advertised or trademarked the word "sriracha.")

When and where did Waffle House open? — 1955, Avondale Estates, Georgia (Labor Day weekend, by neighbors Joe Rogers Sr. and Tom Forkner. FEMA informally tracks storm severity by whether the local Waffle House is open — the Waffle House Index.)

LocalWhich two household staples — one for drains, one for windows — were invented by a Cincinnati company? — Drano and Windex (both from The Drackett Company of Cincinnati: Drano in 1923, Windex in 1933. The business was later sold to Bristol-Myers and then S.C. Johnson.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceSharks are older than trees. Sharks show up in the fossil record around 450 million years ago; the first true trees around 390 million. They are also older than Saturn's rings and the North Star.
  • GeographyScotland's national animal is the unicorn — heraldically official since the 1400s, and still on the royal coat of arms, chained, because a free unicorn was considered too dangerous to handle.
  • Did You KnowA penguin at Edinburgh Zoo outranks most soldiers. Sir Nils Olav III, a king penguin, is a mascot of the Norwegian King's Guard — knighted in 2008 and promoted to major general in 2023, inspecting the troops each time they visit.
  • TravelThe world's shortest scheduled flight runs between Westray and Papa Westray in Orkney: 1.7 miles, timetabled at two minutes, routinely flown in under 90 seconds.
  • FoodPeanuts are not nuts. They are legumes, related to peas and beans, and they ripen underground. Almonds, cashews and pistachios aren't nuts either — they're seeds of drupes, like a peach pit.
  • LanguageThe Hawaiian alphabet has 13 letters — five vowels, seven consonants, and the ʻokina, a glottal stop that counts as its own consonant.
  • OddityVatican City has the highest crime rate per capita on Earth — not because it is lawless, but because millions of tourists get pickpocketed in a country with a few hundred residents in the denominator.
  • HistoryWealthy Europeans avoided tomatoes for about 200 years, convinced they were poisonous. The likely culprit: acidic tomato juice leaching lead out of the pewter plates only rich people ate from.
  • ScienceA blue whale's heart weighs around 400 pounds. When the animal dives, the heart can slow to as few as two beats per minute, then race back to roughly 37 at the surface.
  • LocalCincinnati created America's first fully paid professional fire department in 1853, after volunteer companies spent as much energy brawling with each other over turf as they did putting out fires. It also fielded the first steam fire engines in regular service.
  • LanguageA "jiffy" is a real unit of time. In electronics it is one hundredth of a second; in physics it is the time light takes to cross one fermi — about 3×10⁻²⁴ seconds.
