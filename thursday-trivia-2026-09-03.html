🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Norway?
Oslo — it was called Christiania (later Kristiania) from 1624 until 1925, when the old Norse name was restored.

What is the capital of Georgia (the country)?
Tbilisi — the name means roughly "warm place," after the sulfur hot springs the city was founded on.

What is the capital of Laos?
Vientiane — sits right on the Mekong, across the river from Thailand.

What is the capital of Chad?
N'Djamena — founded by the French in 1900 as Fort-Lamy; renamed in 1973.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who wrote The Metamorphosis (1915), which opens with a man waking up as an insect?
Franz Kafka — he asked his friend Max Brod to burn all his manuscripts. Brod published them instead.

Which painting gave Impressionism its name?
Impression, Sunrise (1872) by Claude Monet — a hostile critic coined the term as an insult.

Whose 1913 ballet premiere caused a riot in a Paris theater?

Who wrote Jane Eyre (1847)?

Who wrote The Sound and the Fury (1929)?

"Whistler's Mother" is a nickname. What is the painting's actual title?

🏭 Founded & Invented

What company launched on this exact date in 1995, and under what name?
eBay — launched as "AuctionWeb" by Pierre Omidyar on Labor Day weekend, Sept. 3, 1995. The first item sold was a broken laser pointer, for $14.83.

Who perfected the modern zipper, and where did the name come from?
Gideon Sundback, 1913 — he called it a "separable fastener." B.F. Goodrich coined "zipper" in 1923 for a line of rubber galoshes.

Who invented Super Glue, and by accident how?
Harry Coover, Eastman Kodak, 1942 — he was trying to make clear plastic gunsights and kept ruining lab equipment. It went on sale in 1958.

What company did the Hassenfeld Brothers found in 1923 — and what phrase did it invent?
Hasbro (Providence, RI) — it started in textile remnants, and coined "action figure" in 1964 so boys' parents wouldn't think G.I. Joe was a doll.

Who invented the stay-tab on a beverage can?
Daniel Cudzik at Reynolds Metals, 1975 — replacing Ermal Fraze's 1959 detachable pull-tab, which was littering beaches and cutting feet.

LocalWhere does the name Fifth Third Bank come from?
A 1908 Cincinnati merger of the Fifth National Bank and the Third National Bank. "Third Fifth" was reportedly avoided because of Prohibition-era connotations.

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceWater can boil and freeze at the same time — it's called the triple point, and it happens at a specific temperature and pressure.
  • SpaceUranus is tipped over on its side (~98° axial tilt), so each pole gets roughly 42 years of continuous sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness.
  • GeographyThe Pacific Ocean is shrinking by a few centimeters a year while the Atlantic grows — the plates are still moving.
  • AnimalsSloths can hold their breath longer than dolphins — about 40 minutes versus roughly 10.
  • BodyYour body produces roughly 25 million new cells every second.
  • FoodMost "wasabi" served in restaurants is dyed horseradish. Real wasabi loses its heat about 15 minutes after it's grated.
  • Language"Muscle" comes from the Latin musculus — "little mouse" — because a flexing bicep looked like one moving under the skin.
  • HistoryNapoleon wasn't short. He was about 5'7", slightly above average for his time; the myth came from French inches being longer than English ones.
  • MathA sheet of paper folded 42 times would reach the Moon. (Folding doubles the thickness each time — you can't actually do it, but the math holds.)
  • SportsEvery baseball used in an MLB game is rubbed with Lena Blackburne Baseball Rubbing Mud, dug from one secret spot on a New Jersey tributary of the Delaware River. The location is a family secret.
  • Pop CultureThe modern image of Santa Claus — round, red-suited, jolly — was largely locked in by illustrator Haddon Sundblom for Coca-Cola ads starting in 1931.
  • WeatherThat smell after rain has a name: petrichor, coined in 1964 by two Australian scientists. It's oils from plants plus a soil bacteria compound called geosmin.
  • LocalCincinnati's Findlay Market (1855) is the oldest continuously operated public market in Ohio.
  • Reds bar-betFrom 1953 to 1958 the team officially went by the "Cincinnati Redlegs" — the front office wanted distance from the word "Reds" during the McCarthy-era Red Scare.
