📍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Canada?

Ottawa — not Toronto or Montreal. Queen Victoria picked it in 1857 precisely because it was a compromise: a lumber town on the border between Ontario and Quebec, and far enough from the U.S. to be defensible.

2. What is the capital of Turkey?

Ankara — not Istanbul. Atatürk moved it inland in 1923 when the republic was founded.

3. What is the capital of Belize?

Belmopan — purpose-built inland after Hurricane Hattie flattened Belize City in 1961. With about 20,000 people it's one of the smallest national capitals in the world.

4. What is the capital of Switzerland?

Bern — and it's a trick: Switzerland has no official capital in its constitution. Bern is designated the "federal city," which is why so many people wrongly guess Zurich or Geneva.

🎨 Arts & Literature

1. Who wrote The Canterbury Tales?

Geoffrey Chaucer — begun around 1387 in Middle English, and left unfinished. Only 24 of the planned 100+ tales exist.

2. Who painted Girl with a Pearl Earring (c. 1665)?

Johannes Vermeer — sometimes called "the Mona Lisa of the North." Fewer than 40 paintings are attributed to him.

3. Who composed The Four Seasons (1725)?

Antonio Vivaldi — a Venetian priest nicknamed "the Red Priest" for his hair. Each concerto came with its own sonnet.

4. Which novel opens: "It was a bright cold day in April, and the clocks were striking thirteen"?

Nineteen Eighty-Four (1949) — George Orwell, whose real name was Eric Blair.

5. Who wrote the epic poem Paradise Lost (1667)?

John Milton — completely blind by then, he dictated the whole thing, mostly to his daughters. He sold it for £10.

6. Who wrote The Handmaid's Tale (1985)?

Margaret Atwood — her rule was that nothing in it could be invented; every practice in Gilead had a real historical precedent.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. Who invented the Twinkie, and when?

James Dewar, 1930, at Continental Baking in Illinois — to keep idle shortcake pans busy outside strawberry season. The original filling was banana; it switched to vanilla during WWII banana rationing and never switched back.

2. Who founded Chuck E. Cheese?

Nolan Bushnell, 1977 — the same man who founded Atari. He wanted somewhere to put arcade cabinets where kids could legally hang out.

3. Who patented the three-position traffic signal?

Garrett Morgan, 1923 — a Black inventor from Cleveland whose key addition was the third "all-stop" warning position. He sold the patent to General Electric for $40,000.

4. What year was Toyota founded, and why isn't it "Toyoda"?

1937 — spun off from Toyoda Automatic Loom Works, named for founder Kiichiro Toyoda. They changed the "d" to a "t" because Toyota takes eight brush strokes in katakana, and eight is a lucky number.

5. Where did the name "Super Bowl" come from?

Chiefs owner Lamar Hunt — he coined it offhandedly after watching his kids play with a Super Ball. The NFL thought it was undignified; it stuck anyway, and became official for Super Bowl III.

6. LocalWhich world-famous Cincinnati pottery was founded in 1880 by a woman?

Rookwood Pottery — founded by Maria Longworth Nichols Storer, making it one of the first large American manufacturing companies owned and run by a woman. Its tile still lines the Carew Tower and NYC subway stations.
