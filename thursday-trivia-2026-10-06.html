📅 This Day in History — October 6

  • 1927 — The Jazz Singer premieres in New York. It was the first feature film with synchronized spoken dialogue, using Warner Bros.' Vitaphone sound-on-disc system. Al Jolson's first spoken line: "Wait a minute, wait a minute, you ain't heard nothin' yet!" Studio co-founder Sam Warner, who pushed for sound, died the day before the premiere.
  • 1866 — The Reno Gang pulls America's first peacetime train robbery, near Seymour, Indiana, about 90 miles west of Cincinnati. They took about $13,000 from an Ohio & Mississippi Railroad express car. 160 years ago today.
  • 1976 — Gerald Ford's debate gaffe. In the second debate with Jimmy Carter he said "there is no Soviet domination of Eastern Europe." 50 years ago today.

Also today: Egypt and Syria launched the Yom Kippur War in 1973. In 1981 Egyptian president Anwar Sadat was assassinated at the parade marking its anniversary. And in 1683, 13 families from Krefeld landed in Philadelphia and founded Germantown, which is why today is German-American Day.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Egypt? — Cairo (Africa's largest metro area. A big satellite city, "6th of October City," is named for the date of the 1973 Suez Canal crossing, which is 53 years ago today. Egypt is also building a new administrative capital in the desert east of Cairo.)

What is the capital of Pakistan? — Islamabad (not Karachi, the largest city and the first capital. Islamabad was built from scratch in the 1960s to a master plan by Greek planner Constantinos Doxiadis, right next to the older city of Rawalpindi.)

What is the capital of Seychelles? — Victoria (on the island of Mahé. It's one of the world's smallest capitals, in Africa's least-populated country.)

What is the capital of Dominica (not the Dominican Republic)? — Roseau (the "Nature Isle" has a flooded fumarole called the Boiling Lake, and parts of Pirates of the Caribbean 2 and 3 were filmed there. The Dominican Republic's capital is Santo Domingo.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played both identical cousins, Patty and Cathy Lane, on The Patty Duke Show (1963–66)? — Patty Duke (she had already won an Oscar at 16 for playing Helen Keller in The Miracle Worker. The theme song explains the setup: "they're cousins, identical cousins.")

Who played Arthur "The Fonz" Fonzarelli on Happy Days (1974–84)? — Henry Winkler (the 1977 episode where Fonzie water-skis over a shark gave us the phrase "jumped the shark." Winkler didn't win his first Emmy until 2018, for Barry.)

Who played Uncle Phil, the judge Philip Banks, on The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air (1990–96)? — James Avery (he also voiced Shredder on the 1987 Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles cartoon.)

Who played brooding rich kid Dylan McKay on Beverly Hills, 90210? — Luke Perry (90210 is a real ZIP code. Perry was in his mid-20s when he started playing a high-school junior.)

Who played the cigar-smoking fighter pilot Lt. Starbuck on the original Battlestar Galactica (1978–79)? — Dirk Benedict (he went on to play smooth-talking Templeton "Faceman" Peck on The A-Team. One episode slipped in a Cylon walking past him as an in-joke.)

Deeper cut: who was the puppeteer and voice of the furry alien on ALF (1986–90)? — Paul Fusco (he also co-created the show. ALF stands for "Alien Life Form." His real name was Gordon Shumway, he came from the planet Melmac, and he loved to eat cats.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which Swiss-French architect, born on this day in 1887, said "a house is a machine for living in"? — Le Corbusier (his real name was Charles-Édouard Jeanneret. His best-known house is the Villa Savoye outside Paris, and he later appeared on Switzerland's 10-franc note.)

Which British novelist, who died yesterday at 86, wrote Kane and Abel (1979)? — Jeffrey Archer (the novel follows two men born on the same day in 1906, one a Boston banker's heir and one a Polish immigrant who becomes a hotel tycoon.)

Which German-American painter created Washington Crossing the Delaware (1851)? — Emanuel Leutze (he painted it in Düsseldorf, reportedly using the Rhine as his model river. The flag in the painting is wrong: the Stars and Stripes weren't adopted until 1777, after the 1776 crossing. Fitting for German-American Day.)

Which French composer wrote The Carnival of the Animals (1886) but banned public performances of it during his lifetime? — Camille Saint-Saëns (he thought it was too frivolous. The only movement he allowed to be published while he was alive was "The Swan.")

Who wrote and illustrated The Little Prince (1943)? — Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (a French pilot, he wrote it in exile in New York, where it was first published. He disappeared on a reconnaissance flight over the Mediterranean in 1944.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who is credited with the first coin-operated vending machine? — Hero of Alexandria, 1st century AD (a coin dropped onto a tray tipped a lever and released a measured squirt of holy water in a temple.)

What sound system did The Jazz Singer (premiered on this day in 1927) use? — Vitaphone (it was a sound-on-disc system: the audio played from big records synced to the projector. Western Electric developed it, and Warner Bros. bet the studio on it. Within a few years sound was printed directly on the film instead.)

In which city did Vernon Rudolph open the first Krispy Kreme in 1937? — Winston-Salem, North Carolina (at first he only sold to grocery stores. People smelled the doughnuts frying and asked to buy them hot, so he cut a hole in the wall and sold them through it.)

What gave George Ballas the idea for the Weed Eater string trimmer in 1971? — The spinning brushes at a car wash (his first prototype in Houston was a popcorn can with pieces of fishing line pushed through holes, spun by an edger motor.)

National Taco Day: who patented a frying form for pre-shaped hard taco shells (granted 1950)? — Juvencio Maldonado (he owned a Mexican restaurant in New York City. His form let kitchens fry shells ahead of time, which helped lead to the fast-food crunchy taco.)

LocalWhich skin-care brand started as a soap company in Cincinnati in 1882? — Jergens (the Andrew Jergens Co.) (Jergens Lotion made the company a household name. Japan's Kao bought it in 1988, and its U.S. headquarters is still in Cincinnati.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • BooksMad Hatter Day is 10/6 because of the Hatter's hat. In John Tenniel's illustrations for Alice in Wonderland, the hat's tag reads "In this Style 10/6," meaning a price of 10 shillings and sixpence.
  • ScienceAbout 100 trillion neutrinos, mostly from the Sun, pass through your body every second. They almost never hit anything, which is why IceCube needed a cubic kilometer of ice to catch a few.
  • FoodTacos al pastor come from Lebanese shawarma. Lebanese immigrants brought vertical-spit roasting to Puebla, Mexico, and Mexican cooks switched the lamb to marinated pork.
  • FoodMarco Polo did not bring pasta to Italy. Italian records mention pasta before he came home from China in 1295, including a 1279 Genoese will that leaves someone a basket of macaroni.
  • GeographyAustralia is wider than the Moon. It's about 4,000 km (2,500 mi) east to west, and the Moon is about 3,474 km (2,159 mi) across.
  • AnimalsCats can't taste sweetness. The gene for one of the two proteins in the sweet-taste receptor doesn't work in cats.
  • BodyYou can't hum with your nose pinched shut. Humming pushes air out through your nose, so once it's blocked the sound stops within a few seconds.
  • Language"OK" started as a joke. In 1839 the Boston Morning Post used it as an abbreviation for a deliberate misspelling, "oll korrect."
  • HistoryA popular myth says David Rice Atchison was president for one day, March 4, 1849, because Zachary Taylor wouldn't be sworn in on a Sunday. Atchison's own Senate term had also ended, and he said he slept through most of the day.
  • LocalThe Bengals' tiger-striped helmets debuted in 1981, the season they reached their first Super Bowl.
