📅 This Day in History — August 12

  • 1981 — The IBM PC goes on sale. Model 5150, about $1,565 to start. IBM built it out of off-the-shelf parts to get it out fast, which let clone makers copy it — and that accident is why "PC" still means what it means. 45 years ago today.
  • 1990 — "Sue" is discovered. Fossil hunter Susan Hendrickson spots bones eroding out of a cliff in the South Dakota badlands: the largest, most complete Tyrannosaurus rex ever found. She got the naming rights; the skeleton now anchors Chicago's Field Museum.
  • 1898 — Hawaii is formally annexed by the United States, in a transfer-of-sovereignty ceremony at Iolani Palace. The same day, an armistice protocol ended the fighting in the Spanish-American War.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented Velcro, and what gave him the idea? — George de Mestral (1941, patented 1955) — burdock burrs stuck to his dog after a hunt. The name is velours + crochet.

Who patented riveted blue jeans in 1873? — Jacob Davis & Levi Strauss — Davis was the Reno tailor with the idea; Strauss had the money for the patent.

Where and when was Microsoft founded? — Albuquerque, New Mexico, 1975 — Gates and Allen, near the maker of the Altair 8800.

Super Glue was discovered by accident while trying to make what? — Harry Coover, 1942 — clear plastic gun sights. He rejected it twice for sticking to everything before selling it in 1958.

Who invented the Slinky? — Richard James, 1943 — a naval engineer who knocked a tension spring off a shelf and watched it "walk." His wife Betty found the name in the dictionary.

What does IKEA stand for? — Ingvar Kamprad, Elmtaryd, Agunnaryd — his name, his family's farm, his village. He founded it in 1943 at age 17.
