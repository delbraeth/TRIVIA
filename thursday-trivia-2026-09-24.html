📅 This Day in History — September 24

  • 1991 — Nirvana releases Nevermind (35 years ago today). DGC pressed 46,251 copies, expecting a modest alt-rock record. It knocked Michael Jackson's Dangerous off No. 1 in January and has sold more than 30 million worldwide.
  • 1869 — "Black Friday," the gold panic. Jay Gould and Jim Fisk tried to corner the U.S. gold market; when the Treasury dumped $4 million in gold onto the market, the price collapsed in minutes and took a chunk of Wall Street with it.
  • 1968 — 60 Minutes premieres on CBS. Harry Reasoner and Mike Wallace hosted; it aired every other Tuesday at first, and is now the longest-running prime-time program in U.S. television history.

Also today: the Judiciary Act of 1789 created the Supreme Court (237 years); Ben Johnson ran 9.79 in Seoul in 1988 and was stripped of the gold three days later; the National Museum of African American History and Culture opened on the Mall in 2016 (10 years).

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Belarus? — Minsk (leveled almost completely in WWII; rebuilt as a showcase of Stalinist architecture)

What is the capital of Kiribati? — South Tarawa (Kiribati is the only country on Earth sitting in all four hemispheres — and the first to greet each new year)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which Baroque painter invented tenebrism — figures lit like a spotlight against near-black shadow — and fled Rome in 1606 after killing a man? — Caravaggio (he died at 38; his body was never found)

Which Dutch painter boiled his work down to black grid lines with red, blue and yellow rectangles? — Piet Mondrian (he called it Neoplasticism; his last, unfinished canvas was Victory Boogie Woogie)

Who wrote The Catcher in the Rye (1951)? — J.D. Salinger (he published nothing after 1965 and lived another 45 years)

Which Russian composer wrote his Piano Concerto No. 2 after hypnotherapy broke a three-year creative collapse? — Sergei Rachmaninoff (he dedicated it to the doctor, Nikolai Dahl)

Who wrote "Stop all the clocks, cut off the telephone" — the poem read in Four Weddings and a Funeral? — W.H. Auden (its real title is "Funeral Blues"; it began as satire in a 1936 play)

Which sculptor carved Apollo and Daphne and designed the great colonnade around St. Peter's Square? — Gian Lorenzo Bernini (he was doing commissioned portrait busts by his teens)

🏭 Founded & Invented

When was Honda Motor Company founded? — September 24, 1948 (78 years ago today, by Soichiro Honda; it started by bolting surplus war-radio engines onto bicycles and is now the world's largest maker of motorcycles)

Who invented the shipping container? — Malcom McLean, 1956 (a North Carolina trucker; the first container voyage, on the Ideal X, cut loading costs from roughly $5.83 a ton to about 16 cents)

Who invented the disposable safety razor? — King C. Gillette, patented 1904 (he also wrote a utopian book proposing one giant company house everyone in North America in a single city powered by Niagara Falls)

Where did Jell-O come from? — 1897, LeRoy, New York (cough-syrup maker Pearle Wait couldn't sell it and gave up the rights for $450; the buyer built it into a multimillion-dollar brand within a decade)

Who trademarked "Aspirin" — and what else did that company trademark the same year? — Bayer, 1899 (Bayer also sold a cough suppressant under a brand name it coined: Heroin. Germany lost both trademarks in the Treaty of Versailles.)

Local: When was Christian Moerlein Brewing founded in Cincinnati? — 1853 (a Bavarian blacksmith turned brewer in Over-the-Rhine; by the 1890s it was Ohio's largest brewery and the first American beer to pass Germany's purity standard for export. Prohibition killed it in 1919; the name was revived in 1981.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceSaturn's rings may be younger than the dinosaurs — Cassini's mass measurements point to an age of roughly 100–400 million years, and they are slowly raining down into the planet.
  • ScienceDiamond and graphite are both pure carbon, and graphite is the more stable form. Every diamond on Earth is very slowly turning into pencil lead.
  • GeographyThe most remote spot on Earth is Point Nemo in the South Pacific — 1,670 miles from the nearest land. When the Space Station passes overhead, the closest humans are in orbit, not on the water.
  • AnimalsThe mantis shrimp punches with the acceleration of a .22 bullet — fast enough to boil the water around its claw into a collapsing bubble that emits a flash of light.
  • BodyHumans have roughly the same density of hair follicles as chimpanzees. Ours are just thin, short and pale — we are not less hairy, only less obviously so.
  • FoodThe Caesar salad is Mexican. Italian-American restaurateur Caesar Cardini threw it together in Tijuana on July 4, 1924, when a holiday rush emptied his kitchen — no lettuce-and-anchovy tradition, just what was left.
  • LanguageA "contronym" is a word that is its own opposite: cleave (split / cling to), sanction (permit / punish), dust (remove dust / apply it), oversight.
  • HistoryAustralia lost the Great Emu War of 1932. Soldiers with two machine guns were sent against emus raiding wheat fields; the birds scattered and regrouped, the campaign was called off, and the emus kept the wheat.
  • MathBenford's Law: in most real-world number sets, about 30% begin with the digit 1 and only about 5% with 9. Forensic accountants use it to spot invented figures, because people making up numbers spread them evenly.
  • SportsDick Fosbury won the 1968 Olympic high jump going over the bar backwards, a style coaches had told him to abandon. Within 12 years nearly every elite high jumper in the world used the Fosbury Flop.
  • Pop CultureDecca Records turned down The Beatles on January 1, 1962, reportedly telling Brian Epstein that "guitar groups are on the way out." They signed Brian Poole and the Tremeloes instead — same audition day, cheaper travel from London.
  • WeatherIt can snow when it is above freezing, but it essentially never snows below about −40°. Air that cold holds almost no moisture.
  • LocalCincinnati once ran five hillside inclines — cable-hauled platforms that carried streetcars, horses and wagons straight up the bluffs starting in 1872. They are why Mount Adams, Price Hill and Clifton became neighborhoods at all. The last one, the Mount Adams incline, shut down in 1948.
