📅 This Day in History — September 24

  • 1991 — Nirvana releases Nevermind (35 years ago today). DGC pressed 46,251 copies, expecting a modest alt-rock record. It knocked Michael Jackson's Dangerous off No. 1 in January and has sold more than 30 million worldwide.
  • 1869 — "Black Friday," the gold panic. Jay Gould and Jim Fisk tried to corner the U.S. gold market; when the Treasury dumped $4 million in gold onto the market, the price collapsed in minutes and took a chunk of Wall Street with it.
  • 1968 — 60 Minutes premieres on CBS. Harry Reasoner and Mike Wallace hosted; it aired every other Tuesday at first, and is now the longest-running prime-time program in U.S. television history.

Also today: the Judiciary Act of 1789 created the Supreme Court (237 years); Ben Johnson ran 9.79 in Seoul in 1988 and was stripped of the gold three days later; the National Museum of African American History and Culture opened on the Mall in 2016 (10 years).

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Belarus? — Minsk (leveled almost completely in WWII; rebuilt as a showcase of Stalinist architecture)

What is the capital of Kiribati? — South Tarawa (Kiribati is the only country on Earth sitting in all four hemispheres — and the first to greet each new year)

Eswatini — formerly Swaziland — has two capitals. Name either. — Mbabane (administrative) or Lobamba (legislative and royal; home of the Queen Mother)

What is the capital of Trinidad and Tobago? — Port of Spain (birthplace of the steelpan; and today, Sept 24, is the country's Republic Day)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played skin-diver Mike Nelson on Sea Hunt (1958–61)? — Lloyd Bridges (father of Jeff and Beau; all three networks passed, so it ran in syndication — and became one of the biggest syndicated hits ever)

Who played Emma Peel on The Avengers (1961–69)? — Diana Rigg (the name came from production shorthand: "man appeal," or M-appeal)

Who played matriarch Victoria Barkley on The Big Valley (1965–69)? — Barbara Stanwyck (a major film star of the '30s and '40s who did her own riding and won an Emmy for it)

Who played Rerun Stubbs on What's Happening!! (1976–79)? — Fred Berry (a dancer with The Lockers before he acted; the red beret was his own idea)

Who played Mike Seaver on Growing Pains (1985–92)? — Kirk Cameron (Leonardo DiCaprio joined in the final season as Luke, a homeless teen the Seavers take in)

Who played rumpled Atlanta defense attorney Ben Matlock (1986–95)? — Andy Griffith (the light-gray suit was a running gag — the character was too cheap to buy another)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who painted Nighthawks (1942), the late-night diner? — Edward Hopper (the diner has no door; Hopper said he was "unconsciously painting the loneliness of a large city")

Who wrote War and Peace? — Leo Tolstoy (he insisted it was "not a novel"; his wife Sophia is said to have copied the manuscript out by hand several times)

Which novel opens "Call me Ishmael"? — Moby-Dick, Herman Melville (1851) (it sold about 3,200 copies in his lifetime; Melville died largely forgotten in 1891)

Who composed The Four Seasons? — Antonio Vivaldi (each concerto came with a sonnet, possibly written by Vivaldi himself, describing what the music depicts)

Who wrote "Do not go gentle into that good night"? — Dylan Thomas (a villanelle, written for his dying father)

Who was the first Black woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1993)? — Toni Morrison (she was a Random House editor for years before publishing her own first novel at 39)

🏭 Founded & Invented

When was Honda Motor Company founded? — September 24, 1948 (78 years ago today, by Soichiro Honda; it is now the world's largest maker of motorcycles)

Who invented the modern ballpoint pen? — László Bíró, Hungarian journalist, patented 1938 (he noticed newspaper ink dried instantly; the British still call the pen a "biro")

Who invented bubble gum, and why is it pink? — Walter Diemer, 1928 (a 23-year-old Fleer accountant tinkering after hours; pink was the only food dye on the shelf, and the color stuck industry-wide)

Who invented the shipping container? — Malcom McLean, 1956 (a North Carolina trucker; the first container voyage, on the Ideal X, cut loading costs from about $5.83 a ton to 16 cents)

Who invented the three-point seat belt? — Nils Bohlin, Volvo, 1959 (Volvo opened the patent to every other automaker for free — one of the great corporate giveaways in history)

Local: When was Procter & Gamble founded in Cincinnati? — 1837 (William Procter, a candlemaker, and James Gamble, a soapmaker — brothers-in-law who married sisters and were pushed into business together by their father-in-law)

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceSaturn's rings may be younger than the dinosaurs — Cassini's measurements point to an age of roughly 100–400 million years, and they are slowly raining down into the planet.
  • ScienceDiamond and graphite are both pure carbon, and graphite is the more stable form. Every diamond on Earth is very slowly turning into pencil lead.
  • GeographyAlaska is the westernmost, easternmost and northernmost U.S. state — the Aleutian chain crosses the 180th meridian into the Eastern Hemisphere.
  • AnimalsSharks are older than trees. Sharks show up in the fossil record around 450 million years ago; the first true trees about 385 million.
  • BodyHumans have roughly the same density of hair follicles as chimpanzees. Ours are just thin, short and pale — we are not less hairy, only less obviously so.
  • FoodRipe cranberries bounce. Growers still sort them on "bounce boards," a machine patented in the 1880s by a grower named John "Peg Leg" Webb, who noticed the good berries bounced down his barn stairs.
  • Language"Nice" originally meant ignorant — from Latin nescius. It drifted through "fussy," "shy" and "precise" before settling on "pleasant."
  • HistoryAustralia lost the Great Emu War of 1932. Soldiers with two Lewis machine guns were sent against emus raiding wheat crops; the birds scattered, the campaign was abandoned, and the emus kept the wheat.
  • MathA sheet of paper folded 42 times would reach the Moon — doubling 0.1 mm 42 times gives about 440,000 km. You cannot actually fold it that many times, which is the other half of the bet.
  • SportsThe marathon is 26.2 miles because of the 1908 London Olympics: the course was stretched so it could start at Windsor Castle and finish in front of the royal box.
  • Pop CultureJames Earl Jones went uncredited as the voice of Darth Vader in the first two Star Wars films — at his own request. He called it "special effects."
  • WeatherIt can snow above freezing, but it essentially never snows below about −40°. That air is too cold to hold enough moisture to make snow.
  • LocalCincinnati has roughly 400 sets of public outdoor stairs — several miles of city-maintained "step streets" built to get people up and down the hillsides before cars. No other American city has anything close per capita.
