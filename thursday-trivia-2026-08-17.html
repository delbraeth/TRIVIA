🕊️ Notable Deaths — Past 7 Days

  • Tommy John, 83 — the left-hander whose name became a surgery. He won 288 games over 26 seasons for the White Sox, Dodgers, Yankees and Angels, made four All-Star teams, and threw his last big-league pitch at 46 years old. In 1974 Dr. Frank Jobe rebuilt his elbow ligament with a tendon from his own arm — a procedure now performed on thousands of pitchers a year and known universally by his name. Died Saturday, Aug 15.
  • Hayden Panettiere, 36 — the actress known for Heroes (Claire Bennet), ABC's Nashville (Juliette Barnes) and the Scream franchise (Kirby Reed). Found dead in a home in Greenville, South Carolina, on Aug 16; police reported no signs of foul play and the cause has not been released. She had published a memoir, This Is Me: A Reckoning, earlier this year.
  • Ruby Andrews, 79 — Chicago soul singer, best known for the 1967 hit "Casanova (Your Playing Days Are Over)" plus "You Made a Believer (Out of Me)" and "Everybody Saw You." Died Aug 13.

📅 This Day in History — August 17

  • 1807 — Robert Fulton's steamboat sets out. The North River Steam Boat (later nicknamed the Clermont) left New York for Albany, 150 miles up the Hudson, and made the round trip on schedule. Steamboats existed before it; this was the first one that made money, which is why Fulton gets the credit.
  • 1969 — Woodstock's final scheduled day. The festival at Max Yasgur's dairy farm in Bethel, N.Y. was supposed to close Sunday night, Aug 17. Rain and delays pushed the last set into Monday morning, when Jimi Hendrix played to about 30,000 people left of the 400,000-plus who had come.
  • 1978 — First transatlantic balloon crossing. The Double Eagle II, crewed by Ben Abruzzo, Maxie Anderson and Larry Newman, landed in a barley field outside Paris after 137 hours from Presque Isle, Maine.
  • 1982 — The first commercial compact disc is pressed. At a Philips plant in Germany. The album was ABBA's The Visitors. Forty-four years ago today.

  • Colombia earthquake toll keeps climbing. The magnitude 7.4 quake that struck near San José del Palmar in western Colombia on Aug 10 — the country's most powerful this century — has now killed at least 288 people, with more than 200 still missing. More than 11 inches of rain east of Indianapolis pushed the White River past its 1913 record, cresting near 24.9 feet at Noblesville — a foot above a mark that had stood 113 years. At least seven deaths are linked to the storms. Gov. Mike Braun declared a state of emergency, called out the National Guard and requested federal aid. Waters began receding over the weekend.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented Coca-Cola, and when? — John Pemberton, 1886 (an Atlanta pharmacist; his bookkeeper Frank Robinson named it and wrote the script logo)

Post-it Notes came from what mistake? — A failed adhesive (Spencer Silver made a too-weak glue at 3M in 1968; Art Fry used it to keep bookmarks in his church hymnal. Launched nationwide 1980.)

When was Ford Motor Company founded? — 1903, Detroit (Henry Ford's third try at a car company; the Model T came in 1908)

Who invented Teflon? — Roy Plunkett, DuPont, 1938 (another accident — a gas cylinder he thought was empty had polymerized into white powder)

What was Amazon originally called? — Cadabra (Bezos founded it in 1994 in a Bellevue, WA garage; a lawyer misheard "Cadabra" as "cadaver," so he changed it)

🧩 General Trivia

  • HistoryCleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid. The pyramid was already ~2,500 years old when she was born.
  • GeographyAfrica is the only continent that sits in all four hemispheres — north, south, east and west.
  • ScienceUnder certain conditions hot water can freeze faster than cold water. It's called the Mpemba effect, named for a Tanzanian schoolboy who noticed it making ice cream in 1963.
  • FoodA pineapple plant takes about two years to produce a single fruit.
  • AnimalsA group of owls is a "parliament."
  • SpaceJupiter's Great Red Spot is shrinking. A century ago it was roughly three Earths wide; it's now down to about one.
  • LanguageThe word "nerd" first appeared in print in Dr. Seuss's If I Ran the Zoo (1950), as the name of a made-up animal.
  • SportsBasketball's first hoops in 1891 were literal peach baskets — and somebody had to climb a ladder and fetch the ball after every single score.
  • Pop CultureSean Connery wore a toupee in every James Bond film he made. He started going bald in his early twenties.
  • Did You KnowThe Eiffel Tower was meant to be temporary — a 20-year permit, then demolition. It survived because it made an excellent radio antenna.
  • BodyA 2014 study estimated the human nose can distinguish roughly a trillion different smells — not the "10,000" figure everyone repeats, which came from a 1927 back-of-envelope guess.
  • LocalCincinnatiCincinnati Music Hall was built on top of a paupers' cemetery. Human remains have turned up during renovations more than once — including during the big 2016–17 restoration, which is a large part of why the building has its haunted reputation.
