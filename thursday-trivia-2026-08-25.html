📅 This Day in History — August 25

  • 1916 — Woodrow Wilson signs the Organic Act creating the National Park Service. 110 years ago today. The parks already existed; what didn't exist was anyone to run them.
  • 1875 — Matthew Webb, a 27-year-old merchant navy captain, becomes the first person to swim the English Channel — 21 hours 45 minutes, greased in porpoise oil, sustained by beef tea and brandy.
  • 1835 — The Great Moon Hoax begins: The New York Sun runs the first of six articles claiming an astronomer had discovered bat-winged humanoids and unicorns living on the Moon. Circulation soared; the paper never formally retracted it.
  • 1944 — The Liberation of Paris is completed as the German garrison surrenders, ending four years of Nazi occupation.
  • 1991 — A 21-year-old Finnish student named Linus Torvalds posts to a Usenet group: "I'm doing a (free) operating system (just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu)." That was Linux. 35 years ago.
  • 2012 — Neil Armstrong dies at 82.

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceHoney never spoils. Archaeologists have found 3,000-year-old honey in Egyptian tombs that was still edible — low moisture, high acidity, and natural hydrogen peroxide make it inhospitable to bacteria.
  • GeographyCanada holds roughly 62% of the world's lakes — more than every other country on Earth combined.
  • SpaceSaturn would float. Its average density (0.69 g/cm³) is less than water's — the only planet in the solar system that could theoretically float in a big enough bathtub.
  • AnimalsA group of hedgehogs is an "array."
  • AnimalsWombat droppings are cube-shaped. Their intestines have varying elasticity that molds the pellets into cubes so they don't roll off the rocks wombats use to mark territory.
  • BodyYour brain is about 2% of your body weight but consumes ~20% of your oxygen and calories.
  • FoodNutmeg is a hallucinogen in large doses — myristicin is genuinely toxic in quantity. A couple of tablespoons will make you very sorry.
  • Language"Queue" is the only common English word that sounds exactly the same when you remove the last four letters.
  • SportsThe marathon's 26.2 miles isn't ancient — the distance was fixed at the 1908 London Olympics so the race could start at Windsor Castle and finish in front of the royal box.
  • HistoryThe last widow of a Civil War veteran died in 2020. Helen Viola Jackson was 101; she had married a 93-year-old Union veteran in 1936, when she was 17.
  • Pop CultureThe voice of Mickey Mouse married the voice of Minnie Mouse. Wayne Allwine and Russi Taylor wed in 1991 and were together until his death in 2009.
  • LocalCincinnati Union Terminal's rotunda is the Hall of Justice. Hanna-Barbera's designers modeled the Super Friends headquarters on the 1933 art deco station — which now houses the Cincinnati Museum Center.
  • LocalRod Serling learned his trade in Cincinnati. Before The Twilight Zone, he wrote for WLW and WKRC here in the early 1950s — and quit in 1955 in disgust at how networks and sponsors kept sanding the edges off his scripts. Science fiction turned out to be the loophole.
