📍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Ireland?

Dublin — from the Irish Dubh Linn, "black pool." Founded as a Viking settlement around 841.

2. What is the capital of Romania?

Bucharest — home of the Palace of the Parliament, the heaviest building on Earth.

3. What is the capital of Armenia?

Yerevan — founded 782 BC as the fortress of Erebuni, making it older than Rome.

4. What is the capital of Honduras?

Tegucigalpa — officially the twin city "Tegucigalpa, M.D.C.," merged with Comayagüela.

🎨 Arts & Literature

1. Who painted The Arnolfini Portrait (1434), with its famous convex mirror?

Jan van Eyck — he signed the wall itself: "Jan van Eyck was here."

2. Who wrote The Call of the Wild (1903)?

Jack London — drawn from his own year in the Klondike gold rush.

3. "Mother died today. Or maybe yesterday, I don't know." opens which novel?

The Stranger (1942) — Albert Camus, Nobel laureate 1957.

4. Which poet of the Harlem Renaissance wrote "I, too, sing America" and "Harlem" ("What happens to a dream deferred?")?

Langston Hughes.

5. Who wrote the play The Importance of Being Earnest (1895)?

Oscar Wilde — it premiered weeks before the libel trial that destroyed him.

6. Who composed the six Brandenburg Concertos (1721)?

Johann Sebastian Bach — dedicated to a margrave who seems never to have had them performed; the manuscript sat unpublished for well over a century.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. How did 7-Eleven get its name?

Founded 1927 in Dallas as the Southland Ice Company; renamed in 1946 for its then-radical hours, 7 a.m. to 11 p.m.

2. Who invented Silly Putty?

GE engineer James Wright, 1943, while chasing a synthetic rubber substitute. It had no war use; ad man Peter Hodgson bought it, put it in plastic eggs in 1950, and died rich.

3. Who invented the pop-up toaster?

Charles Strite, 1919 — a Minnesota factory mechanic fed up with burnt cafeteria toast. Bread came pre-sliced only from 1928 on.

4. Who invented Scotch tape, and why is it called "Scotch"?

Richard Drew at 3M, 1930. An irritated auto painter told him to take the tape back to his "Scotch" bosses and put more adhesive on it — 3M kept the insult as a brand.

5. Who made the first manned hot air balloon flight?

The Montgolfier brothers' balloon, Paris, November 1783 — after a test flight carrying a sheep, a duck and a rooster.

6. LocalWhat is the oldest professional observatory in the United States?

The Cincinnati Observatory (1843). Former president John Quincy Adams, 76, traveled here to lay the cornerstone — the last public speech of his life. Mount Adams is named for him.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceThere is no permanent "dark side of the Moon." The far side gets just as much sunlight — it simply never faces Earth.
  • ScienceMetal doesn't feel colder than wood at the same temperature. It just pulls heat out of your hand faster.
  • GeographyRussia and the United States are about 2.4 miles apart — Big Diomede and Little Diomede islands, separated by the International Date Line. Locals call them Tomorrow Island and Yesterday Isle.
  • AnimalsSharks are older than trees. Sharks go back roughly 450 million years; the first trees, about 350 million.
  • BodyYour body produces enough heat in 30 minutes to bring a half-gallon of water to a boil — if you could capture all of it.
  • FoodPound cake is named for the original recipe: one pound each of butter, sugar, eggs and flour.
  • Language"Uncopyrightable" is the longest common English word with no repeated letters — 15 of them.
  • HistoryBetty White was born before sliced bread. She arrived in January 1922; commercially sliced bread went on sale in 1928.
  • MathZero has no Roman numeral. The system had no symbol for nothing.
  • SportsAn average NFL broadcast contains about 11 minutes of actual live-ball action across three-plus hours.
  • Pop CultureThe Simpsons' Springfield is named after Springfield, Oregon — near Matt Groening's hometown of Portland.
  • WeatherRaindrops aren't teardrop-shaped. Falling air resistance flattens them into something closer to a hamburger bun.
  • LocalCincinnati has nearly 400 sets of public outdoor staircases built into its hillsides — one of the largest municipal step systems in the country.
  • Reds bar betThe 1990 Reds are the only team in National League history to lead their division wire-to-wire from opening day — and they then swept the heavily favored Oakland A's in the World Series.
