🕯️ Notable Deaths — Past Week

  • Tom Skerritt, 93 (Oct 21). Captain Dallas in Alien, Viper in Top Gun, and Sheriff Jimmy Brock on Picket Fences, which won him a 1993 Emmy. He also played Sally Field's husband in Steel Magnolias and the minister father in A River Runs Through It. He died of natural causes.
  • Todd Benzinger, 63 (Oct 21). 🔴 Reds first baseman, 1989–91, who caught the last out of the 1990 World Series. He grew up in Dayton, Kentucky, went to New Richmond High, and later managed the Dayton Dragons.
  • Ron Duguay, 69 (Oct 22). Long-haired, helmetless Rangers star of the late '70s who played in the 1979 Stanley Cup Final, had 274 NHL goals, and later called Rangers games on MSG. He died after a battle with Stage 4 cancer.
  • John Anderson, 71 (Oct 21). Country Hall of Famer who sang "Swingin'" and "Seminole Wind."
  • Chuck Amato, 80 (Oct 19). Head football coach at NC State and longtime Florida State assistant.
  • Also: Cornell Green, 86 (Oct 21), Cowboys Pro Bowl defensive back; Washington Post film critic Ann Hornaday, 66; Brazilian singer-songwriter Benito di Paula, 84; and former Broncos GM John Beake, 87.

📜 This Day in History — October 23

  • 1956 — The Hungarian Revolution began (70 years ago today). A student march in Budapest grew into a national uprising, and crowds pulled down a giant statue of Stalin. Soviet tanks crushed the revolt by Nov. 10.
  • 1993 — Joe Carter won the World Series with a walk-off homer. Toronto trailed 6–5 in the 9th of Game 6 when his three-run shot off Phillies closer Mitch "Wild Thing" Williams made it 8–6. It's still the only Series ended by a come-from-behind walk-off home run.
  • 2001 — Steve Jobs unveiled the iPod (25 years ago today). It had a 5 GB drive and cost $399, pitched as "1,000 songs in your pocket."
  • Also: the Second Battle of El Alamein began (1942). The Smurfs first appeared in Belgium's Spirou magazine (1958). A truck bomb at the Beirut Marine barracks killed 241 U.S. service members (1983). Born today: Johnny Carson (1925), Pelé (1940), Michael Crichton (1942), "Weird Al" Yankovic (1959), Ryan Reynolds (1976).

🧩 General Trivia

  • NatureThe swallows of Mission San Juan Capistrano traditionally leave on Oct 23 and return on March 19, St. Joseph's Day. They winter about 6,000 miles away, around Goya, Argentina.
  • AnimalsSnow leopards can't roar. They chuff, hiss and mew, and they wrap their thick, nearly body-length tails around their faces like a scarf.
  • SportsPelé was born Edson Arantes do Nascimento, named after Thomas Edison.
  • SportsRon Duguay is tied for the fastest goal to start a game in Rangers history: 9 seconds in, on April 6, 1980.
  • MoviesTom Skerritt's first film, War Hunt (1962), was also the screen debut of Robert Redford, who directed him 30 years later in A River Runs Through It.
  • ComicsThe first Clayface (Detective Comics #40, 1940) was Basil Karlo, a washed-up horror-movie actor, which fits Horror Movie Day.
  • TVJohnny Carson hosted The Tonight Show for 30 years (1962–92). He grew up in Norfolk, Nebraska, and performed magic as a teenager as "The Great Carsoni."
  • Music"Weird Al" Yankovic got his start mailing homemade tapes to radio host Dr. Demento as a teenager. His accordion lessons began the day before his 7th birthday.
  • FoodBoston cream pie is a cake: sponge layers with custard filling and chocolate glaze. It's the official state dessert of Massachusetts. Its state doughnut is the Boston cream.
  • ScienceA mole of donuts would cover the Earth about 5 miles deep. Avogadro's number is that big.
  • TechApple sold roughly 450 million iPods before discontinuing the last model, the iPod touch, in May 2022.
