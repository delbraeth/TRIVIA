📅 This Day in History — August 28

  • 1963 — The March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom draws about 250,000 people to the Lincoln Memorial, where Martin Luther King Jr. delivers the "I Have a Dream" speech. The most-quoted portion was improvised — gospel singer Mahalia Jackson called out "Tell them about the dream, Martin!" and King left his prepared text. 63rd anniversary.
  • 1922 — The first radio commercial airs on WEAF in New York: the Queensboro Realty Corporation paid $50 for ten minutes to pitch apartments in Jackson Heights, Queens. Herbert Hoover, then Commerce Secretary, had said it was "inconceivable" that broadcasting would be "drowned in advertising chatter."
  • 2005 — Hurricane Katrina strengthens to Category 5 in the Gulf and New Orleans Mayor Ray Nagin issues the city's first-ever mandatory evacuation order. Landfall came the next morning.

📍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Bhutan?

Thimphu — the only national capital in the world with no traffic lights (they installed one, hated it, and went back to a white-gloved policeman).

2. What is the capital of Iceland?

Reykjavík — the northernmost capital of a sovereign state, and its name means "smoky bay" for the steam from its hot springs.

3. What is the capital of Ecuador?

Quito — sits at about 9,350 ft and in 1978 became one of the first two cities ever named a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

4. What is the capital of Vietnam?

Hanoi — not Ho Chi Minh City, which is larger but is not the capital.

🎨 Arts & Literature

1. Who wrote Frankenstein (1818)?

Mary Shelley — she was 18 when she started it, during the rainy "year without a summer" at Lake Geneva.

2. Who painted The Scream (1893)?

Edvard Munch — Norwegian; there are four versions, two of which have been stolen and recovered.

3. Who composed "In the Hall of the Mountain King," from the Peer Gynt suite?

Edvard Grieg (1875) — written as incidental music for an Ibsen play.

4. Who wrote Things Fall Apart (1958), the most widely read African novel?

Chinua Achebe — the title comes from Yeats's poem "The Second Coming."

5. Which American poet wrote the ever-expanding collection Leaves of Grass?

Walt Whitman — he revised and republished it for nearly 40 years, from 1855 to his death.

6. Who wrote the play Death of a Salesman (1949)?

Arthur Miller — Pulitzer Prize for Drama, 1949. Protagonist: Willy Loman.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. What year was Harvard founded?

1636 — the oldest university in the United States, founded 140 years before the country was.

2. Who invented the modern ballpoint pen?

László Bíró, a Hungarian journalist, patented in 1938 — he'd noticed newspaper ink dried fast and wanted a pen that did the same. In Britain and Australia they're still just called "biros."

3. Who discovered Teflon, and how?

Roy Plunkett at DuPont, 1938 — by accident. A canister of refrigerant gas had polymerized into a slippery white waxy solid overnight.

4. Where does the name Crayola come from?

Binney & Smith, 1903 — from the French craie (chalk) plus "oleaginous" (oily). The first box of eight cost a nickel.

5. When and by whom was Sony founded?

1946, Tokyo — Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita, originally as Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo. Renamed Sony in 1958.

6. LocalWhich company that still prints Bicycle playing cards was founded in Cincinnati?

The United States Playing Card Company — traces to 1867 in Cincinnati; Bicycle brand launched 1885. The plant sat in Norwood for decades before moving to Kentucky in 2009.
