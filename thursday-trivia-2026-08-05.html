🌍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Malawi?

Lilongwe (replaced Zomba as capital in 1975; Blantyre is the largest commercial city)

2. What is the capital of Nicaragua?

Managua (sits on the shore of Lake Managua; leveled by earthquakes in 1931 and 1972)

3. What is the capital of Montenegro?

Podgorica (called Titograd from 1946 to 1992; Cetinje remains the ceremonial "Old Royal Capital")

4. What is the capital of Palau?

Ngerulmud (the least-populated national capital on Earth — a few hundred people; moved from Koror in 2006)

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. Who invented the first portable fire extinguisher, and roughly when?

George William Manby, 1818 (his "Extincteur" was a copper vessel of potassium carbonate solution pushed out by compressed air)

2. Which company built the vehicle that became known as the Jeep for the US Army in World War II?

Willys-Overland (American Bantam produced the 1940 prototype; Willys and Ford mass-produced it from 1941)

3. Who invented the modern pin tumbler lock, still the standard for house keys today?

Linus Yale Jr., patented 1861–65 (building on his father's design; the Yale name is still on billions of locks)

4. In what year was Hasbro founded, and by whom?

1923, by the Hassenfeld Brothers in Providence, Rhode Island (they started in textile remnants; their Mr. Potato Head was the first toy ever advertised on TV, in 1952)

5. Who invented the first whirlpool bath pump, giving the world the Jacuzzi?

Candido Jacuzzi, 1956 (he built it to treat his young son's rheumatoid arthritis; the family firm originally made aircraft propellers)

6. Who invented instant ramen, and in what year?

Momofuku Ando, 1958 (his flash-frying method produced "Chicken Ramen"; Cup Noodles followed in 1971)

🧠 General Trivia

  • ScienceHelium is the only element discovered in space before it was found on Earth — spotted in the Sun's spectrum during an 1868 eclipse, which is why it's named for Helios.
  • GeographyNauru, the world's smallest island country, has no official capital at all. Government offices sit in the district of Yaren, which everyone treats as the capital by default.
  • FoodRipe cranberries bounce. Growers historically graded them on "bounce boards" — a good berry clears about four inches; a bad one just thuds.
  • HistoryHarvard University (founded 1636) is older than calculus, which Newton and Leibniz developed independently in the 1660s and '70s.
  • SportsAn Olympic gold medal is mostly silver. Rules require only about 6 grams of pure gold plating over a sterling silver core.
  • Pop CultureNintendo's Mario debuted in Donkey Kong (1981) as a carpenter named "Jumpman." The plumber job and the name came later.
  • AnimalsA group of ladybugs is called a "loveliness."
  • LanguageThe word "dord" appeared in Webster's New International Dictionary from 1934 to 1947 and meant nothing. A slip labeled "D or d" (abbreviations for density) was misread as a single word — the most famous "ghost word" in English.
  • SpaceSaturn's moon Enceladus fires plumes of water ice hundreds of miles out of cracks near its south pole — and that spray is what forms Saturn's faint E ring.
  • Did You KnowThe world's oldest continuously operating hotel is Nishiyama Onsen Keiunkan in Japan, open since 705 AD and run by 52 generations of the same family.
  • HistoryThe Great Molasses Flood of January 1919: a storage tank in Boston's North End burst, sending 2.3 million gallons of molasses through the streets in a wave moving about 35 mph. It killed 21 people.
  • SciencePound for pound, human bone has a compressive strength roughly four times that of concrete.
