📅 This Day in History — September 16

  • 1620 — The Mayflower sails from Plymouth, England with 102 passengers. It was the third attempt; the leaky Speedwell had already forced them back twice.
  • 1810 — Father Miguel Hidalgo rings the bell at Dolores and issues the Grito de Dolores, starting the Mexican War of Independence. Mexico's Independence Day is today, not Cinco de Mayo.
  • 1893 — The Cherokee Strip land run: more than 100,000 people sprint into a 6-million-acre strip of Oklahoma at noon. Largest land run in history.
  • 1908 — General Motors is founded in Flint, Michigan by William C. Durant — as a holding company that initially owned only Buick.
  • 1920 — A horse-drawn wagon packed with dynamite explodes on Wall Street across from the J.P. Morgan building, killing 38. Never solved; the pockmarks are still visible on 23 Wall Street.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who painted The Raft of the Medusa (1819)? — Théodore Géricault (he interviewed survivors and studied corpses to get it right; he was 27)

Who wrote "The Rime of the Ancient Mariner"? — Samuel Taylor Coleridge (source of "an albatross around your neck" and "water, water, everywhere")

Who wrote The Count of Monte Cristo and The Three Musketeers? — Alexandre Dumas (both serialized in 1844; his father was a general born enslaved in Saint-Domingue)

Which composer wrote Turandot, home of "Nessun dorma"? — Giacomo Puccini (he died before finishing it; at the premiere Toscanini stopped where Puccini's writing ended)

Who wrote the 1879 play A Doll's House? — Henrik Ibsen (the slam of Nora's door was called "the door slam heard round the world")

Who wrote Dune (1965)? — Frank Herbert (rejected about 20 times; finally published by an auto-repair-manual house)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Where does the name "Jeep" come from? — Disputed — most likely a slurring of the Army designation "GP" (general purpose), though many credit Eugene the Jeep, the magic creature from the Popeye comics. Willys-Overland trademarked it in 1943.

Who invented the orange traffic cone? — Charles D. Scanlon, a Los Angeles street painter, patented in 1943 — he was tired of drivers plowing through his wet paint.

When and where was Fisher-Price founded? — 1930, East Aurora, New York — by Herman Fisher, Irving Price and Helen Schelle, in the depths of the Depression.

Who invented the graham cracker? — Rev. Sylvester Graham, c. 1829 — a Presbyterian minister who preached that bland whole-grain food would curb carnal urges. S'mores were not the plan.

Where did the Dixie Cup get its name? — From a neighbor — Hugh Moore's 1907 disposable "Health Kup" shared a building with the Dixie Doll Company; he liked the word and borrowed it in 1919.

🏙️ LOCAL: When was the Cincinnati Enquirer founded? — 1841 — it has published continuously ever since, making it one of the oldest daily newspapers in the United States still in print.

🧠 General Trivia

  • ScienceHelium is the only element discovered in space before it was found on Earth — spotted in the Sun's spectrum during an 1868 eclipse, 27 years before anyone isolated it here. It's named for Helios.
  • SpaceNeptune has completed exactly one orbit since it was discovered in 1846 — it finished in 2011. A Neptunian year is about 165 Earth years.
  • GeographyRussia and China are tied for the most land neighbors — 14 countries each.
  • AnimalsKoalas have fingerprints so similar to human ones that they've reportedly muddled forensic analysis at Australian crime scenes.
  • BodyStomach acid runs around pH 1–2 — corrosive enough to dissolve a razor blade. The lining survives by replacing itself constantly.
  • FoodNutmeg and mace come from the same fruit: mace is the red lacy webbing wrapped around the nutmeg seed. Two spices, one pit.
  • Language"Robot" comes from the Czech robota — forced labor — coined for Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R. His brother Josef actually suggested the word.
  • HistoryFrance's last execution by guillotine was in 1977 — the same year Star Wars opened and the Apple II went on sale.
  • SportsThe NBA didn't adopt the three-point line until the 1979–80 season, and it was widely dismissed as a gimmick. Boston's Chris Ford hit the league's first one.
  • Pop CultureNintendo's Mario is named after Mario Segale, the landlord of Nintendo of America's Seattle-area warehouse, who reportedly came by demanding overdue rent.
  • WeatherThose bright spots flanking a low sun are "sun dogs" — technically parhelia — caused by flat hexagonal ice crystals bending light 22°.
  • LocalCincinnati's 1830s nickname was "Porkopolis" — it was the largest pork-packing city in the country, and herds of hogs were driven through the streets to the slaughterhouses.
  • Reds bar-betThe Big Red Machine's 1975–76 titles make the Reds the last National League team to win back-to-back World Series — fifty years and counting.
