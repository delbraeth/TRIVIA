📅 This Day in History — September 8

  • 1966 — Star Trek premieres on NBC. Sixty years ago today, with the episode "The Man Trap." It ran three seasons, was cancelled, and then became one of the largest franchises in entertainment through syndication. Hence Star Trek Day.
  • 1974 — Gerald Ford pardons Richard Nixon. A "full, free, and absolute pardon" for any offenses committed in office, one month after Nixon resigned. Ford's approval rating fell more than 20 points almost overnight, and many historians point to it as the reason he lost in 1976.
  • 1900 — The Galveston hurricane makes landfall. Roughly 8,000 dead, still the deadliest natural disaster in U.S. history. Galveston afterward raised the entire city — thousands of buildings jacked up by hand — and built a seawall.
  • 1504 — Michelangelo's David is unveiled in the Piazza della Signoria in Florence. He was 29, and the block of marble had been abandoned by two earlier sculptors.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who painted Las Meninas (1656)? Diego Velázquez — he painted himself into it, at the easel.

Who wrote Heart of Darkness (1899)? Joseph Conrad — Polish-born, and he didn't speak fluent English until his twenties.

Who composed Eine kleine Nachtmusik and an unfinished Requiem? Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

"Mrs. Dalloway said she would buy the flowers herself." Which novel — and who wrote it? Mrs Dalloway (1925), Virginia Woolf

Who painted Christina's World (1948)? Andrew Wyeth — the model was his neighbor Christina Olson, who had a degenerative muscular disorder.

Which pointillist painted A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte? Georges Seurat — it took him two years; it hangs in Chicago.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented modern air conditioning, and why? Willis Carrier, 1902 — not for comfort, but to control humidity at a Brooklyn printing plant where paper kept swelling and ruining color registration.

Where does the word "escalator" come from? It was a trademark — Charles Seeberger and Otis coined it around 1900; the courts later ruled it had become a generic term. Jesse Reno patented an early moving stairway in 1892 as a Coney Island ride.

When was McDonald's founded — and when did Ray Kroc get involved? 1940 / 1955 — Richard and Maurice McDonald opened in San Bernardino in 1940; Kroc, a milkshake-mixer salesman, opened his first franchise in Des Plaines, Illinois in 1955 and bought them out in 1961.

Bluetooth is named after whom? Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson — the 10th-century Danish king who united Denmark and Norway. The logo is his initials in Norse runes.

Who invented Kool-Aid, and where? Edwin Perkins, Hastings, Nebraska, 1927 — he dehydrated a liquid concentrate so it could ship cheaply in envelopes.

LocalWhen did the Cincinnati Zoo open? 1875 — the second-oldest zoo in the United States, after Philadelphia's. It's also where Martha, the last passenger pigeon, died in 1914.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceA teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh roughly 6 billion tons on Earth.
  • GeographyIstanbul is the only major city on two continents — the Bosphorus splits it between Europe and Asia.
  • AnimalsTardigrades have survived direct exposure to the vacuum of space, on a 2007 European Space Agency mission — and came back and reproduced.
  • BodyBabies are born with about 300 bones; adults have 206. Many fuse together, especially in the skull and spine.
  • FoodKetchup was sold as medicine in the 1830s — an Ohio physician marketed tomato pills as a cure for indigestion and jaundice.
  • Language"Typewriter" can be typed using only the top row of a QWERTY keyboard — reportedly a salesman's demo trick.
  • HistoryThe Eiffel Tower was supposed to be torn down after 20 years. It survived because the French military found it made an excellent radio antenna.
  • SportsOlympic gold medals are mostly silver. The last solid-gold golds were awarded in 1912; today's are about 1.2% gold by weight.
  • Pop Culture"Happy Birthday to You" was under claimed copyright until 2016, when a federal judge and a $14 million settlement pushed it into the public domain.
  • Did You KnowThe shortest commercial flight in the world — Westray to Papa Westray in Orkney — takes about 90 seconds.
  • LocalCincinnati's Ingalls Building (1903) was the world's first concrete-frame skyscraper. Sixteen stories, at Fourth and Vine. Critics were so certain it would collapse that a reporter reportedly stayed up all night waiting to watch it fall. It's still standing.
  • Reds bar-betHank Aaron's 714th home run — the one that tied Babe Ruth — was hit in Cincinnati. Opening Day, April 4, 1974, first swing of the season, off Reds pitcher Jack Billingham at Riverfront Stadium.
