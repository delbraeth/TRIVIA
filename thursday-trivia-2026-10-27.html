📜 This Day in History — October 27

  • 1787 — The first Federalist essay was published in New York's Independent Journal, under the pen name "Publius." Hamilton, Madison and Jay wrote 85 of them to sell the new Constitution.
  • 1904 — New York's subway opened. The IRT line ran 9.1 miles from City Hall to 145th Street, with 28 stations. Mayor George McClellan drove the first train himself.
  • 2004 — The Red Sox won the World Series for the first time since 1918, finishing a 4-game sweep of the Cardinals in St. Louis during a total lunar eclipse. Tonight, Milwaukee can finish a sweep of its own.
  • Also: The Mets won Game 7 of the 1986 World Series (1986). Saturn I, the first rocket in the Saturn family, flew for the first time (1961). Congo was renamed Zaire (1971). Turkmenistan declared independence (1991). Born today: Captain James Cook (1728), Niccolò Paganini (1782), Theodore Roosevelt (1858), Dylan Thomas (1914), Ruby Dee (1922), Roy Lichtenstein (1923), Sylvia Plath (1932), John Cleese (1939).
