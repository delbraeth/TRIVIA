📰 Current Events — This Week

  • Nobel Prize in Chemistry (announced this morning): Henri Kagan, 95, of France and Kenso Soai of the Tokyo University of Science won for "mirror-image" chemistry. Many molecules come in left- and right-handed versions, and drugs often need just one. They found ways that a small imbalance can snowball into a nearly pure batch of one form. 🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Andorra? — Andorra la Vella (at about 1,023 m (3,356 ft), it's the highest capital in Europe. Andorra has two heads of state: the president of France and the Bishop of Urgell in Spain.)

What is the capital of the Solomon Islands? — Honiara (on Guadalcanal, scene of the 1942–43 WWII campaign. John F. Kennedy's PT-109 was sunk in the Solomons in 1943.)

What is the capital of Grenada? — St. George's (the "Isle of Spice" has a nutmeg on its flag. The U.S. invaded in October 1983.)

What is the capital of Niger (not Nigeria)? — Niamey (on the Niger River, which gives both countries their names. Niger is the largest country in West Africa by area.)

LocalWhich famous jukebox and organ company started in Cincinnati in 1856? — Wurlitzer (German immigrant Rudolph Wurlitzer started by importing instruments. The company later made "Mighty Wurlitzer" theater organs and jukeboxes, built in North Tonawanda, N.Y.)

National Frappe Day: the Greek iced-coffee frappé was invented by accident in 1957 at which event? — The Thessaloniki International Fair (a Nestlé employee, Dimitris Vakondios, couldn't find hot water for his instant coffee, so he shook it with cold water.)

National Propane Day: who identified propane in gasoline in 1910? — Walter Snelling (a U.S. Bureau of Mines chemist who wondered why gasoline evaporated so fast. He later sold his propane patent for $50,000.)

National LED Light Day: which three scientists won the 2014 Nobel in Physics for the bright blue LED? — Isamu Akasaki, Hiroshi Amano, Shuji Nakamura (blue was the missing color. Without it there was no white LED light.)

Chocolate Covered Pretzel Day: where did Julius Sturgis open America's first commercial pretzel bakery in 1861? — Lititz, Pennsylvania (the bakery still operates and gives tours where you can try twisting your own pretzel.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • ScienceSpearmint and caraway are the same molecule. Both get their smell from carvone. One is the left-handed version and the other is the right-handed mirror image. That's the "handedness" behind today's chemistry Nobel.
  • MedicineThalidomide is the classic example of why handedness matters. One mirror form was the sedative and the other was linked to birth defects. Worse, the body converts one into the other.
  • SciencePropane has no smell. The rotten-egg odor is a chemical called ethyl mercaptan, added so people can notice a leak.
  • WordsIn chemistry, meth-, eth-, prop- and but- aren't number words, which is why propane (3 carbons) isn't "tri-ane." Greek counting only starts at pent- (5): pentane, hexane, octane.
  • FoodStarbucks didn't invent the Frappuccino. It bought the name in 1994 when it acquired Boston's Coffee Connection chain, which had made the drink first.
  • FoodOne story says monks invented the pretzel as a reward for children who said their prayers. The twist is meant to look like arms crossed over the chest.
  • TechMost "white" LED bulbs are really blue LEDs coated with yellow phosphor. Our eyes see blue plus yellow as white.
  • GeographyAndorra has no airport and no train station. Visitors drive in from Spain or France.
  • AnimalsStarfish have no blood and no brain. They pump filtered seawater through their bodies instead. Marine biologists prefer the name "sea star," because they aren't fish.
  • NobelAt 95, Henri Kagan is among the oldest Nobel winners ever. The record belongs to John Goodenough, who was 97 when he shared the 2019 chemistry prize for the lithium-ion battery.
