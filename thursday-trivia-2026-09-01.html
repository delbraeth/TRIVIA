📅 This Day in History — September 1

1939 — Germany invades Poland at 4:45 a.m., opening the Second World War in Europe. Britain and France declared war two days later. 87th anniversary.

1985 — A joint U.S.–French expedition led by Robert Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel finds the wreck of the Titanic, about 12,500 feet down and 400 miles off Newfoundland. The unmanned sled Argo spotted one of the ship's boilers in the middle of the night — they found the debris field first, not the hull.

1972 — Bobby Fischer becomes world chess champion in Reykjavík when Boris Spassky resigns game 21 by telephone. First American ever to hold the title, and the first non-Soviet champion in 24 years.

1878 — Emma Nutt goes to work for the Boston Telephone Dispatch as the world's first female telephone operator. She stayed on the job 33 years, and the switchboards were all-women within a few years.

📍 Capitals Quiz

1. What is the capital of Portugal?

Lisbon — older than Rome; largely rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake and tsunami.

2. What is the capital of Argentina?

Buenos Aires — "good airs"; its residents are called porteños, "people of the port."

3. What is the capital of Kyrgyzstan?

Bishkek — called Frunze from 1926 to 1991; the name comes from the paddle used to churn fermented mare's milk.

4. What is the capital of Madagascar?

Antananarivo — "the city of the thousand," for the thousand soldiers once posted to guard it.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. Who invented Kevlar?

Stephanie Kwolek at DuPont, 1965 — five times stronger than steel by weight; she was trying to make a lighter tire fiber.

2. Where and when was Starbucks founded, and where did the name come from?

1971, Pike Place Market in Seattle, by a teacher, a history teacher and a writer. Named for Starbuck, the first mate in Moby-Dick. It sold beans, not drinks, for its first decade.

3. Who invented bubble gum, and why is it pink?

Walter Diemer, a 23-year-old Fleer accountant, 1928 (Dubble Bubble). Pink was the only food dye left on the shelf — and it stuck.

4. Who really created Monopoly?

Lizzie Magie patented "The Landlord's Game" in 1904 as an anti-monopoly teaching tool. Charles Darrow sold his version to Parker Brothers in 1935 and got the credit; Magie was paid $500.

5. Who invented the integrated circuit?

Jack Kilby (Texas Instruments, 1958) and, independently, Robert Noyce at Fairchild. Kilby won the Nobel in Physics in 2000.

6. LocalWho founded the Cincinnati Bengals, and when?

Paul Brown, 1967 — the same man who founded and was later fired by the Cleveland Browns, the team named after him.
