📅 This Day in History — August 11

  • 1973 — Hip-hop is born. DJ Kool Herc spins a back-to-school party in the rec room at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx and stretches the drum breaks by cutting between two copies of the same record. That night is now the accepted birthday of hip-hop — which is exactly why today is Hip Hop Day.
  • 1965 — The Watts riots begin in Los Angeles after a traffic stop escalates. Six days of unrest follow, leaving 34 dead and around $40 million in damage.
  • 1992 — The Mall of America opens in Bloomington, Minnesota — then and now the largest mall in the United States, with an indoor amusement park in the middle of it.

🏭 Founded & Invented

What does the "40" in WD-40 stand for? → The 40th formula — "Water Displacement, 40th attempt," cracked in 1953 by the Rocket Chemical Company of San Diego to stop corrosion on the Atlas missile.

Who invented the Band-Aid, and why? → Earle Dickson, 1920 — a Johnson & Johnson cotton buyer whose new wife kept cutting herself in the kitchen. He pre-made bandages so she could dress a wound alone.

Who invented modern air conditioning, and what was it for? → Willis Carrier, 1902 — built to control humidity at a Brooklyn printing plant so the ink would register. Cooling people was a side effect.

What inspired the Frisbee? → Frisbie Pie Company tins — New England students tossed the empty pans; Wham-O bought the "Pluto Platter" in 1957 and renamed it in 1958.

Where does the name "Crayola" come from? → French craie (chalk) + "oleaginous" (oily) — coined by Alice Binney in 1903 for Binney & Smith.

What was America's first fast-food hamburger chain? → White Castle, 1921 — Wichita, Kansas. The white porcelain and stainless steel were deliberate: ground beef had a terrible reputation and they wanted the kitchen to look sterile.
