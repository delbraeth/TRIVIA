📅 This Day in History — October 8

  • 1871 — The Great Chicago Fire starts in the O'Leary family barn on DeKoven Street. It burned for about two days, killed around 300 people and left about 100,000 homeless. 155 years ago today.
  • 1871 — The same night, the Peshtigo Fire swept through northeastern Wisconsin and killed an estimated 1,200 to 2,500 people. It's still the deadliest wildfire in U.S. history, but Chicago got the headlines.
  • 1918 — Cpl. Alvin York, a Tennessee farmer who first sought to be a conscientious objector, led an attack in the Argonne Forest that captured 132 German soldiers. He received the Medal of Honor, and Gary Cooper won an Oscar playing him in Sergeant York (1941).

Also today: Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn was named the Nobel laureate in Literature (1970). He didn't go to Stockholm to accept, fearing the Soviets wouldn't let him back in. Today, Anne Carson won the same prize (see Current Events).

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the official capital of Yemen? — Sana'a (Houthi rebels have held it since 2014, so the internationally recognized government works from the port city of Aden. Sana'a's old city is famous for its multistory mud-brick "tower houses.")

What is the capital of Equatorial Guinea? — Malabo (it's on Bioko Island, not the mainland. Equatorial Guinea is the only African country with Spanish as an official language, and it's building a new capital, Ciudad de la Paz, in the jungle.)

What is the capital of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines? — Kingstown (not Kingston, Jamaica. Much of Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl was filmed on St. Vincent.)

What is the capital of Gabon? — Libreville ("Free Town" in French. It was settled in 1849 by Africans freed from a captured slave ship, much like Freetown, Sierra Leone.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Today's Nobel winner wrote Autobiography of Red (1998), a novel in verse about Geryon, the winged red monster Hercules killed. Name her. — Anne Carson (a classics professor from Toronto who also translated Sappho. In 2001 she became the first woman to win the T.S. Eliot Prize, for The Beauty of the Husband.)

"There was a boy called Eustace Clarence Scrubb, and he almost deserved it." Which book opens this way? — The Voyage of the Dawn Treader — C.S. Lewis (the third Narnia book published, in 1952. Eustace is turned into a dragon.)

Which poet of "Thirteen Ways of Looking at a Blackbird" spent his career as an insurance executive in Hartford? — Wallace Stevens (he became a vice president of the Hartford Accident and Indemnity Co. and won the Pulitzer in 1955.)

Which French composer wrote the gentle piano pieces Gymnopédies (1888)? — Erik Satie (his piece Vexations is marked to be played 840 times. John Cage organized a full performance in 1963 that lasted about 18 hours.)

Which comic novel was published in 1980, 11 years after its author's death, after his mother pushed it on Walker Percy? It won the 1981 Pulitzer. — A Confederacy of Dunces — John Kennedy Toole (its hero, Ignatius J. Reilly, has a statue on Canal Street in New Orleans. The title comes from a Jonathan Swift quote.)

Halloween season: which author created schoolmaster Ichabod Crane and the Headless Horseman? — Washington Irving ("The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" appeared in The Sketch Book (1819–20), with "Rip Van Winkle.")

🏭 Founded & Invented

Fluffernutter Day: who invented Marshmallow Fluff in Somerville, Mass., in 1917? — Archibald Query (he sold it door to door, then sold the recipe in 1920 to Durkee & Mower, which still makes it, for $500.)

Pierogi Day: the first national frozen-pierogi brand, Mrs. T's, was founded in 1952 in which state? — Pennsylvania (Shenandoah) (Ted Twardzik named it for his mother, Mary Twardzik, whose pierogies sold well at church fairs.)

Hurricane Isaias: the 1-to-5 hurricane wind scale is named for an engineer and which National Hurricane Center director? — Herbert Saffir and Robert Simpson (Saffir, the engineer, worked out the wind-damage side in 1969, and Simpson added storm surge in the early 1970s.)

Chicago rebuilt after the 1871 fire. Which 1885 Chicago building is usually called the first skyscraper? — The Home Insurance Building (designed by William Le Baron Jenney with a load-bearing metal frame. It was 10 stories, later 12, and was torn down in 1931.)

The Skydance merger: in what year did Harry, Albert, Sam and Jack incorporate Warner Bros.? — 1923 (their first theater was a nickelodeon in New Castle, Pennsylvania, in the early 1900s.)

LocalWhich "glasses in about an hour" chain did former P&G brand manager Dean Butler found in 1983? — LensCrafters (the first store opened in Florence, Kentucky. Headquarters is in Mason, Ohio.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • HistoryMrs. O'Leary's cow was framed. Reporter Michael Ahern admitted in 1893 that he made up the story of the cow kicking over a lantern. Chicago's City Council officially cleared the O'Learys in 1997.
  • Words"Hurricane" comes from hurakán, a word the Taíno people of the Caribbean used for a storm god.
  • WeatherHurricanes, typhoons and cyclones are the same kind of storm. Only the ocean changes the name. In the Northern Hemisphere they spin counterclockwise.
  • WeatherAtlantic storm names repeat every six years. This year's list reuses 2020's, which is why there's another Isaias. Names of very destructive storms, like 2020's Laura, are retired, and Laura was replaced by Leah.
  • NobelAnne Carson is only the 19th woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, which has gone to 123 laureates since 1901.
  • NobelAll the Nobel Prizes are presented in Stockholm except the Peace Prize, which is awarded in Oslo. Alfred Nobel's will gave that job to a Norwegian committee, when Norway and Sweden were united.
  • FoodThe first marshmallows came from the root of the marsh-mallow plant. Modern ones use gelatin and none of the plant at all.
  • FoodIn 2006, a Massachusetts state senator tried to limit Fluff in school lunches. Another state lawmaker answered by proposing the Fluffernutter as the official state sandwich.
  • SportsAt Pittsburgh Pirates home games, people in giant pierogi costumes race around the field midgame. It's a Pittsburgh tradition since the 1990s.
  • GeographyYemen's Socotra island is home to the umbrella-shaped dragon's blood tree. About a third of its plants grow nowhere else on Earth.
