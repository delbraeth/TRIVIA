📜 This Day in History — November 9

  • 1938 — Kristallnacht, the "Night of Broken Glass." Nazi mobs attacked Jewish homes, shops and synagogues across Germany and Austria over two days. About 30,000 Jewish men were sent to concentration camps.
  • 1965 — The Northeast Blackout left about 30 million people in the U.S. and Ontario without power for up to 13 hours. One faulty relay at a power station near Niagara Falls set it off.
  • 1967 — The first issue of Rolling Stone came out in San Francisco. John Lennon was on the cover, in a still from How I Won the War.
  • 1989 — The Berlin Wall opened. East German spokesman Günter Schabowski said at a press conference that the new travel rules took effect "immediately." Crowds went to the checkpoints that night, and the guards let them through.
  • Also: Cambodia became independent from France (1953). Born today: Ivan Turgenev (1818), Hedy Lamarr (1914), Spiro Agnew (1918), Dorothy Dandridge (1922), Anne Sexton (1928), Carl Sagan (1934), Bob Gibson (1935), Mary Travers (1936), Lou Ferrigno (1951), Eric Dane (1972), Nick Lachey (1973), Sisqó (1978).

🕯️ The 1989 Monday protests that helped bring down the Wall began in Leipzig. Leipzig is the largest city in Saxony, but what is Saxony's capital?
Dresden

🏛️ Jay Rockefeller died this week after serving as West Virginia's governor and senator. What is West Virginia's capital?
Charleston (not Wheeling, which was the first capital)

🧩 General Trivia

  • HistorySchabowski's answer about when the new travel rules started, "sofort, unverzüglich" ("immediately, without delay"), was a mistake. They weren't supposed to take effect until the next day.
  • GeographyThe Berlin Wall ran about 96 miles around West Berlin, and it stood for 28 years (1961–1989).
  • MythA famous 1966 story said births spiked nine months after the 1965 blackout. A 1970 study found no baby boom.
  • ScienceCarl Sagan never said "billions and billions" on Cosmos. It came from Johnny Carson's impression of him, and Sagan later used it as the title of his last book.
  • FoodScrapple is a Pennsylvania Dutch loaf of pork scraps, cornmeal and buckwheat flour, fried in slices. In Pennsylvania Dutch it's called pon haus.
  • FlagsCambodia, independent since this date in 1953, has a building on its flag: Angkor Wat. Very few national flags show one.
  • HockeyThe Ducks have scored five or more goals in eight straight games, the first NHL team to do that since the 1988–90 Kings, Wayne Gretzky's team.
  • FilmThe only live-action movie not in English with a bigger opening than Godzilla Minus Zero is The Passion of the Christ (2004), which is in Aramaic, Hebrew and Latin.
  • GolfBlades Brown played in his first PGA Tour event at 16. He won Sunday at 19.
  • MusicNick and Drew Lachey of 98 Degrees grew up in Cincinnati. Both went to the city's School for Creative and Performing Arts.
