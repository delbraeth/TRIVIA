📜 This Day in History — October 20

  • 1973 — The "Saturday Night Massacre." Nixon ordered Watergate special prosecutor Archibald Cox fired. Attorney General Elliot Richardson and his deputy, William Ruckelshaus, both resigned rather than do it. Solicitor General Robert Bork fired Cox.
  • 1944 — MacArthur wades ashore at Leyte: "People of the Philippines, I have returned." The Battle of Leyte Gulf, probably the largest naval battle in history, began days later.
  • 1973 — On the same day as the firings, Queen Elizabeth II opened the Sydney Opera House. It was 10 years late and cost A$102 million against a A$7 million estimate.
  • Also: a Lynyrd Skynyrd plane crash near Gillsburg, Mississippi, killed Ronnie Van Zant and Steve and Cassie Gaines (1977). The band had released Street Survivors, with flames on the cover, three days earlier. Jacqueline Kennedy married Aristotle Onassis on Skorpios (1968).

🧩 General Trivia

  • FoodFor International Chefs Day: tradition says a chef's tall white toque has 100 pleats, one for each way to cook an egg.
  • FoodChicken and waffles as a dish is usually traced to Harlem's Wells Supper Club in 1938. It fed jazz musicians coming off late gigs, when it was too late for dinner and too early for breakfast.
  • MoviesBela Lugosi was born on this date in 1882, in Lugos, Hungary. He named himself after his hometown and was buried in his Dracula cape in 1956.
  • SportsMickey Mantle was born Oct 20, 1931. His father named him after the Hall of Fame catcher Mickey Cochrane. Mantle still holds the record for World Series home runs, with 18.
  • MusicTom Petty was born Oct 20, 1950, in Gainesville. At age 10 he met Elvis on the Florida set of Follow That Dream (1961).
  • MusicSnoop Dogg (Calvin Broadus Jr.) turns 55 today. His mother called him "Snoopy" because he loved the Peanuts beagle.
  • Did You KnowTo "bork" someone, meaning to block a nominee with a public attack, comes from Robert Bork. He fired Cox in 1973, and the Senate rejected his Supreme Court nomination 58–42 in 1987.
  • GeographyThe Sydney Opera House's sails are covered in 1,056,006 self-cleaning tiles, made in Sweden.
  • GeographyAlberta, which just voted to stay in Canada, has been essentially rat-free since the 1950s. A provincial "rat patrol" guards its eastern border.
  • Did You KnowThe UN's World Statistics Day is held only every five years, on Oct 20 (2010, 2015, 2020, 2025). The next one is in 2030.
  • BaseballThe Rays lost the 2008 World Series to the Phillies and the 2020 Series to the Dodgers. The Brewers lost their only Series, in 1982, to the Cardinals. One of them wins its first title this year.
