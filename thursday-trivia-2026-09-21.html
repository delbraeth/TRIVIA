📅 This Day in History — September 21

  • 1970 — Monday Night Football debuts on ABC (56 years ago today, also a Monday). Browns 31, Jets 21 in Cleveland before 85,000 fans, with Keith Jackson, Don Meredith and Howard Cosell in the booth. Roone Arledge doubled the usual camera count. ABC charged $65,000 a minute — a bargain once the game pulled a 33 share.
  • 1897 — "Yes, Virginia, there is a Santa Claus." The New York Sun runs an unsigned editorial answering 8-year-old Virginia O'Hanlon's letter. It was written under deadline in under 500 words by Francis Pharcellus Church, a Civil War correspondent who reportedly grumbled the whole time. His authorship wasn't revealed until he died in 1906.
  • 1937 — The Hobbit is published by George Allen & Unwin in London. The publisher's 10-year-old son Rayner was paid a shilling to read the manuscript and wrote the report that got it accepted.
  • 1981 — Sandra Day O'Connor is confirmed 99–0 as the first woman on the U.S. Supreme Court. (The one absent senator was Max Baucus, who supported her.)

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Tajikistan?
Dushanbe — the name is Persian for "Monday." The city grew out of a village famous for its Monday marketplace. (Fitting for a sheet dated a Monday.)

What is the capital of Angola?
Luanda — founded by the Portuguese in 1576, and for years ranked among the most expensive cities in the world for expatriates.

What is the capital of Bahrain?
Manama — from the Arabic for "the place of rest." Bahrain is an archipelago of roughly 50 natural islands plus dozens of artificial ones.

What is the capital of Guatemala?
Guatemala City — built after earthquakes destroyed Antigua in 1773, and sitting in a valley ringed by volcanoes, three of them active.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who created Sherlock Holmes?
Arthur Conan Doyle — he killed Holmes off at Reichenbach Falls in 1893 and was pressured into resurrecting him a decade later.

Which composer wrote Also sprach Zarathustra, whose opening became the theme of 2001: A Space Odyssey?
Richard Strauss (1896) — no relation to the waltz Strausses of Vienna.

Which self-taught French painter, a customs officer by trade, painted The Sleeping Gypsy and The Dream?
Henri Rousseau — nicknamed "Le Douanier." He painted jungles he never saw, using the Paris botanical gardens as reference.

Who wrote the 1913 play Pygmalion, later adapted into My Fair Lady?
George Bernard Shaw — the only person to win both a Nobel Prize in Literature and an Academy Award.

Who wrote and illustrated Where the Wild Things Are (1963)?
Maurice Sendak — the monsters were reportedly modeled on his relatives.

Who wrote the 1940 novel Native Son?
Richard Wright — the first novel by a Black author chosen by the Book-of-the-Month Club.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who chose the "@" symbol for email addresses, and when?
Ray Tomlinson, 1971 — he picked it because it was on the keyboard, rarely used in names, and already meant "at."

Who invented the QR code, and what for?
Masahiro Hara at Denso Wave, 1994 — to track auto parts on Toyota assembly lines. Denso chose not to enforce its patent, which is why the format spread everywhere.

When was Home Depot founded, and by whom?
1978, by Bernie Marcus and Arthur Blank — both had just been fired from a California hardware chain. First stores opened in Atlanta in 1979.

What was the first fully enclosed, climate-controlled shopping mall in America?
Southdale Center, Edina, Minnesota, 1956 — designed by Austrian architect Victor Gruen, who later called the American mall a bastardization of his idea.

When did the first commercial lithium-ion battery go on sale?
1991, from Sony — the chemistry's developers, John Goodenough, M. Stanley Whittingham and Akira Yoshino, shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Goodenough was 97, the oldest Nobel laureate ever.

LocalWhen did Frisch's Big Boy start in Cincinnati?
1946 — Dave Frisch licensed the Big Boy name for the Cincinnati territory and opened the first Frisch's Big Boy on Central Parkway. The family's restaurant business dates to a café his father opened in 1905, and Frisch's own tartar sauce became the regional signature.

🧠 General Trivia

  • SpaceSaturn's moon Titan is the only place besides Earth with stable liquid on its surface — lakes and rivers of liquid methane and ethane.
  • BodyYour skeleton completely replaces itself roughly every ten years. The bone you have now is not the bone you had in 2016.
  • GeographyOnly three countries are completely surrounded by a single other country: Lesotho (South Africa), San Marino and Vatican City (both Italy).
  • FoodRhubarb stalks are fine but the leaves are toxic (oxalic acid). In 1947 a U.S. customs court officially reclassified rhubarb as a fruit — purely so importers paid the lower tariff.
  • Language"Strengths" is the longest common English word with only one vowel.
  • Did You KnowThe Eiffel Tower grows roughly six inches taller in summer — the iron expands in the heat — and leans slightly away from the sun.
  • SportsEvery official Major League baseball has exactly 108 double stitches: 216 individual stitches, all sewn by hand.
  • HistoryWoolly mammoths were still alive on Wrangel Island in the Arctic around 1650 BC — by which point the Great Pyramid of Giza was already about a thousand years old.
  • AnimalsElephants are the only mammals that cannot jump. All four feet never leave the ground at once.
  • NatureCertain bamboo species grow up to 35 inches in a single day — fast enough to watch.
  • Pop CultureSesame Street's Count von Count is built on a real folklore trope: vampires were said to suffer from arithmomania, a compulsion to count scattered objects, so you could escape one by throwing seeds on the ground.
  • LocalIn 1827 Cincinnati got the "Time Store," Josiah Warren's experiment in pricing goods by the labor-hours they took to produce. Customers paid in promissory notes for their own labor. It ran three years and turned a profit.
