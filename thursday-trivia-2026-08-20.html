📅 This Day in History — August 20

  • 1920 — Radio and the NFL were both born on this date. In Detroit, station 8MK made what's generally called the first commercial radio broadcast (it later became WWJ). The same day in Canton, Ohio, seven men including Jim Thorpe met to organize a professional football league — the American Professional Football Conference, which became the NFL. Same date. Same year. Two American institutions.
  • 1975 — NASA launches Viking 1, which the following July became the first spacecraft to successfully land on Mars and operate there. It kept transmitting for more than six years.
  • 1968 — The Prague Spring ends as Soviet and Warsaw Pact tanks roll into Czechoslovakia overnight, crushing Alexander Dubček's "socialism with a human face."
  • 1938 — Lou Gehrig hits his 23rd career grand slam, a record that stood for 75 years until Alex Rodriguez passed it in 2013.
  • 1858 — Charles Darwin's theory of natural selection appears in print for the first time, in the journal of the Linnean Society of London — more than a year before On the Origin of Species.

📍 Capitals

1. What is the capital of Myanmar?
Naypyidaw — a purpose-built capital carved out of farmland; the government abruptly moved there from Yangon in 2005. It has 20-lane highways that are usually empty.

2. What is the capital of Bolivia?
Sucre — the constitutional capital and seat of the judiciary. La Paz is the seat of government and where the president lives, so both answers get argued. If the question says "constitutional capital," it's Sucre.

3. What is the capital of Ghana?
Accra — on the Gulf of Guinea, and the closest major capital city on Earth to the intersection of the Equator and the Prime Meridian.

4. What is the capital of Nepal?
Kathmandu — the only city in the world where you'll find seven separate UNESCO World Heritage monument zones inside one valley.

🎨 Arts & Literature

1. Who wrote Wuthering Heights (1847)?
Emily Brontë — published under the pen name "Ellis Bell." It was her only novel; she died the following year at 30.

2. Who painted The Starry Night (1889)?
Vincent van Gogh — painted from the window of the asylum at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, from memory and during the day.

3. "Ride of the Valkyries" comes from which composer's Ring cycle?
Richard Wagner — from Die Walküre. The full four-opera cycle runs about 15 hours and took him 26 years to write.

4. Who wrote Invisible Man (1952)?
Ralph Ellison — winner of the National Book Award. Not to be confused with H.G. Wells's The Invisible Man, which is a different book entirely.

5. Which poet wrote "The Road Not Taken"?
Robert Frost — and the poem is widely misread: the two roads are described as "worn really about the same."

6. Who wrote One Hundred Years of Solitude (1967)?
Gabriel García Márquez — the Colombian Nobel laureate; the novel's fictional town is Macondo.

🏭 Founded & Invented

1. What was bubble wrap originally invented to be?
Textured wallpaper — Alfred Fielding and Marc Chavannes sealed two shower curtains together in 1957. The wallpaper flopped; it was later pitched as greenhouse insulation, and only then as packaging.

2. LocalWhat was Play-Doh originally sold as?
Wallpaper cleaner — made by Kutol Products of Cincinnati. When coal furnaces died out, so did the market; the family repurposed the putty as a children's toy in the mid-1950s.

3. Who invented Coca-Cola, and in what year?
John Pemberton, 1886 — an Atlanta pharmacist. His bookkeeper Frank Robinson named it and wrote the script logo still in use.

4. Where was Microsoft founded, and when?
Albuquerque, New Mexico, 1975 — Bill Gates and Paul Allen set up there to be near MITS, maker of the Altair 8800. They didn't move to Washington until 1979.

5. Who invented the modern three-point seat belt, and what did his employer do with the patent?
Nils Bohlin at Volvo, 1959 — Volvo gave the patent away for free so every carmaker could use it. It's credited with saving over a million lives.

6. Why did Candido Jacuzzi build the first Jacuzzi whirlpool pump?
For his young son's rheumatoid arthritis — a portable pump to give hydrotherapy at home, in 1956. The family had previously made aircraft propellers.
