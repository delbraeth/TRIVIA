📅 This Day in History — October 9

  • 1701 — Yale is founded. Connecticut's legislature chartered the "Collegiate School." It was renamed in 1718 for merchant Elihu Yale, who donated books, goods and a portrait of King George I. 325 years ago today.
  • 1888 — The Washington Monument opens to the public. At 555 feet, it was the world's tallest structure until the Eiffel Tower passed it in 1889. Look for the color change about 150 feet up, where construction stopped for more than 20 years.
  • 1986 — The Phantom of the Opera opens in London at Her Majesty's Theatre, with Michael Crawford and Sarah Brightman. 40 years ago today. It's still running in the West End.

Also today: John Lennon was born in Liverpool in 1940. He'd have been 86. His son Sean was born on his 35th birthday, Oct 9, 1975.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

Today's Nobel winner is from South Africa, which has three capitals. Name them. — Pretoria, Cape Town and Bloemfontein (Pretoria is the seat of the president and government, Cape Town hosts Parliament, and Bloemfontein is home to the Supreme Court of Appeal.)

Leif Erikson Day: what is the capital of Greenland? — Nuuk (Leif's father, Erik the Red, started the Norse settlements in Greenland and picked the name to attract settlers. Greenland is part of the Kingdom of Denmark, and Nuuk is one of the world's northernmost capitals.)

What is the capital of Haiti? — Port-au-Prince (Haiti won independence from France in 1804. It was the first Black-led republic and the first independent country in Latin America and the Caribbean.)

What is the capital of the United Arab Emirates? (Not Dubai.) — Abu Dhabi (Dubai has the Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building at 2,717 feet, but Abu Dhabi is the capital and the largest of the seven emirates.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Leif Erikson Day: an actor named Leif Erickson played rancher "Big John" Cannon on which NBC western (1967–71)? — The High Chaparral (he was born William Anderson, and his stage name was a play on the Norse explorer's. The show was set in 1870s Arizona Territory.)

Ditka tribute: which Cheers actor played Bob Swerski in SNL's "Bill Swerski's Superfans" ("Da Bears!")? — George Wendt (the Superfans worshipped "Da Coach," Mike Ditka. Robert Smigel created the sketch, and Mike Myers and Chris Farley were also in it.)

Who played the title role on Webster (1983–89)? — Emmanuel Lewis (his adoptive TV dad was played by former Detroit Lions lineman Alex Karras. Karras's real-life wife, Susan Clark, played his TV wife.)

Who played young Dr. James Kildare on NBC's Dr. Kildare (1961–66)? — Richard Chamberlain (he later became TV's "King of the Miniseries" in Shōgun (1980) and The Thorn Birds (1983).)

Sci-fi: who played Quark, the Ferengi bartender on Star Trek: Deep Space Nine (1993–99)? — Armin Shimerman (he also played Principal Snyder on Buffy the Vampire Slayer.)

Who played Zack Morris on Saved by the Bell (1989–93)? — Mark-Paul Gosselaar (Zack famously could "freeze time" to talk to the audience. Gosselaar later joined NYPD Blue as Det. John Clark.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Lennon's birthday: what was the title of John Lennon's 1964 book of nonsense stories, poems and drawings? — In His Own Write (it was a bestseller and was often compared to Lewis Carroll and Edward Lear. A second book, A Spaniard in the Works, followed in 1965.)

Which French journalist wrote the 1910 novel The Phantom of the Opera? — Gaston Leroux (the Phantom's name is Erik. Leroux also wrote The Mystery of the Yellow Room, a classic "locked-room" mystery.)

Which 1948 South African novel follows Zulu pastor Stephen Kumalo searching Johannesburg for his son? — Cry, the Beloved Country — Alan Paton (it came out the same year apartheid became official policy. James Earl Jones starred in the 1995 film.)

Which opera, by a composer born this week in 1813, includes the aria "La donna è mobile"? — Rigoletto — Giuseppe Verdi (records disagree on whether Verdi was born Oct 9 or 10. He kept the aria secret from the cast until the final rehearsal so the tune wouldn't leak before the 1851 premiere.)

Which Russian painter gave up a law career at 30, is credited with some of the first purely abstract paintings, and said he could "hear" colors? — Wassily Kandinsky (he likely had synesthesia and named many works "Compositions" and "Improvisations," like music.)

"Where's Papa going with that ax?" Which book opens this way? — Charlotte's Web — E.B. White (1952) (Fern is asking about the runt piglet, Wilbur. White also co-wrote the style guide The Elements of Style.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

World Post Day marks the founding of which organization on Oct 9, 1874, in Bern, Switzerland? — The Universal Postal Union (it was founded as the General Postal Union and renamed in 1878. It's the second-oldest international organization, after the International Telecommunication Union (1865).)

Fire Prevention Week: which U.S. Forest Service mascot was introduced in 1944? — Smokey Bear (he was named for New York assistant fire chief "Smokey" Joe Martin. A real cub rescued from a 1950 New Mexico fire became the living Smokey at the National Zoo.)

Ditka's Bears were founded in 1920 by a starch company in Decatur, Illinois. What were they called? — The Decatur Staleys (George Halas moved them to Chicago in 1921 and renamed them the Bears in 1922. Halas drafted Ditka in 1961 and hired him as coach in 1982.)

Pizza Day: in 1960, brothers Tom and James Monaghan bought a pizza shop called DomiNick's. Where? — Ypsilanti, Michigan (James traded his half for a Volkswagen Beetle. The dots on the Domino's logo stood for the first three stores.)

Lennon's birthday: what company did the Beatles found in 1968, named for a fruit? — Apple Corps (Paul McCartney said the logo was inspired by a René Magritte painting of an apple. Apple Corps sued Apple Computer over the name several times.)

Philadelphia Water Works engineer Frederick Graff Sr. is credited with inventing what, around 1801? — The above-ground fire hydrant (no one can check his patent, because the U.S. Patent Office's records burned in a fire in 1836.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • HistoryLeif Erikson Day is Oct 9 because the sloop Restauration brought the first organized group of Norwegian immigrants to New York on Oct 9, 1825.
  • HistoryThe only confirmed Viking site in North America is L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland. Tree rings in wood cut there show the Norse were there in exactly 1021 AD.
  • FoodTo be called Roquefort, the cheese must be aged in the natural Combalou caves at Roquefort-sur-Soulzon, France. In 1925 it became France's first cheese with protected-origin status.
  • FoodLegend says Pizza Margherita was made in Naples in 1889 for Queen Margherita of Savoy. Tomato, mozzarella and basil match the red, white and green of the Italian flag.
  • SafetyFire Prevention Week is the longest-running public health observance in the U.S. President Coolidge first proclaimed it in 1925, and it always includes Oct 9, the anniversary of the Great Chicago Fire.
  • SportsDitka was the first tight end ever inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame (1988). As a rookie in 1961, he had 1,076 receiving yards, a tight-end rookie record for 63 years.
  • MusicThe 1985 Bears' "Super Bowl Shuffle" was recorded before they'd won the Super Bowl and was nominated for a Grammy. Ditka wasn't in it.
  • MusicJohn Lennon was obsessed with the number 9. He was born Oct 9 and wrote "Revolution 9," "#9 Dream" and "One After 909."
  • BroadwayThe Phantom of the Opera is the longest-running show in Broadway history, with 13,981 performances before it closed in April 2023.
  • WeatherA "major" hurricane is Category 3 or higher, with sustained winds of at least 111 mph.
  • StampsThe United Kingdom is the only country whose postage stamps don't carry its name, because it issued the first one, the Penny Black, in 1840. The monarch's head is enough.
