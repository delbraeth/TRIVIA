  • 1851 — The New York Times publishes its first edition. 175 years ago today. It cost a penny, was called the New-York Daily Times, and the "All the News That's Fit to Print" slogan didn't arrive until 1897.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Chile?
Santiago — founded 1541, ringed by the Andes. Chile's independence is celebrated today, Sept 18.

What is the capital of Bosnia and Herzegovina?
Sarajevo — "the Jerusalem of Europe": a mosque, a synagogue, a Catholic cathedral and an Orthodox church all within a few hundred yards. Hosted the 1984 Winter Olympics.

What is the capital of the Maldives?
Malé — one of the most densely populated cities on Earth, packed onto an island roughly 2 square miles.

What is the capital of Zambia?
Lusaka — named for a village headman, Chief Lusaaka; became the capital in 1935 when it was moved from Livingstone.

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played the title character on The Virginian (1962–71)?
James Drury — his character was never given a name in nine seasons. It was TV's first 90-minute weekly western.

Who played Kato on The Green Hornet (1966–67)?
Bruce Lee — it was his first major American role, and it made him a star in Hong Kong before he was one in the U.S.

Who played Dr. Quincy on Quincy, M.E. (1976–83)?
Jack Klugman — the medical examiner never had a first name spoken on screen either.

Who played Ralph Hinkley, the teacher with the red superhero suit, on The Greatest American Hero (1981–83)?
William Katt — the theme song, "Believe It or Not," hit #2 on the Billboard chart.

Who played Dr. Harry Weston on Empty Nest (1988–95)?
Richard Mulligan — the show was a Golden Girls spinoff, set next door in the same Miami neighborhood.

Who played Wilson W. Wilson Jr., the over-the-fence neighbor on Home Improvement (1991–99)?
Earl Hindman — his face was hidden by the fence for eight seasons; he only revealed it at the final curtain call.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who wrote Doctor Zhivago and was forced to decline the 1958 Nobel Prize in Literature?
Boris Pasternak — Soviet authorities pressured him into refusing; his son accepted it for him in 1989.

Who painted A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882)?
Édouard Manet — his last major work; art historians still argue about whether the mirror's reflection is geometrically possible.

Who wrote Long Day's Journey into Night and won the 1936 Nobel Prize in Literature?
Eugene O'Neill — the only American playwright ever to win the literature Nobel. He asked that the play not be staged until 25 years after his death.

Which novel opens "I write this sitting in the kitchen sink"?
I Capture the Castle (1948), by Dodie Smith — who also wrote The Hundred and One Dalmatians.

Who wrote the 1956 poem "Howl"?
Allen Ginsberg — its publisher, Lawrence Ferlinghetti, was tried for obscenity and won.

Who painted Wanderer above the Sea of Fog (c. 1818)?
Caspar David Friedrich — the defining image of German Romanticism.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented the lava lamp, and when?
Edward Craven Walker, 1963 — a British accountant who got the idea from a homemade egg timer in a pub. He sold it as the "Astro Lamp."

How was the frozen carbonated drink — the ICEE, later licensed as the Slurpee — invented?
By accident, in the late 1950s — Kansas Dairy Queen owner Omar Knedlik's soda fountain broke, so he stashed bottles in the freezer. Customers liked the slush better than the soda. 7-Eleven licensed it in 1965 and named it the Slurpee.

When and where was the first roller coaster in America?
Coney Island, 1884 — LaMarcus Thompson's Switchback Railway. It topped out around 6 mph and cost a nickel.

Who created the first crossword puzzle?
Arthur Wynne, Dec 21, 1913 — in the New York World. He called it a "Word-Cross," and a typesetter's error flipped the name.

When was Chipotle founded?
1993, in Denver — by Steve Ells, a culinary school graduate, using an $85,000 loan from his father. He figured he'd need to sell 107 burritos a day to break even; he was selling over 1,000 within a month.

LocalWhen was Gold Star Chili founded?
1965, Mount Washington — by the four Daoud brothers, Jordanian immigrants who bought a hamburger stand called the Hamburger Heaven and kept the previous owner's chili recipe.

🧠 General Trivia

  • ScienceGlass is not a slow-flowing liquid. Old cathedral windows are thicker at the bottom because crown glass was made by spinning, and glaziers set the heavy edge down.
  • SpaceVenus rotates backward and so slowly that one Venusian day (243 Earth days) is longer than its year (225).
  • GeographyIstanbul is the only major city on two continents — the Bosphorus splits it between Europe and Asia.
  • AnimalsA group of flamingos is a "flamboyance." They're born gray; the pink comes entirely from carotenoids in their diet.
  • BodyThe human nose can distinguish roughly a trillion distinct scents — far more than the "10,000" figure that circulated for 80 years on no real evidence.
  • FoodPineapples aren't from Hawaii. They're native to South America; Columbus found them in the Caribbean, and they didn't reach Hawaii until about 1813.
  • Language"Quarantine" comes from the Venetian quaranta giorni — the forty days ships were held offshore during the plague.
  • HistoryOxford University was already teaching when the Aztec capital Tenochtitlán was founded in 1325. Oxford dates to at least 1096.
  • MathIn a room of 23 people there's better than a 50% chance two share a birthday. At 70 people it's 99.9%.
  • SportsGolf balls originally had smooth surfaces. Players noticed scuffed old balls flew farther, and manufacturers started adding dimples in the 1890s.
  • Pop CultureThe Hollywood sign originally read HOLLYWOODLAND — a 1923 real estate ad meant to last 18 months. The last four letters came down in 1949.
  • WeatherLightning strikes the Earth about 100 times per second, and a bolt heats the surrounding air to roughly 50,000°F — about five times the surface of the sun.
  • LocalCincinnati's Roebling Suspension Bridge (1866) was the longest suspension bridge in the world when it opened. John Roebling used it as the prototype for the Brooklyn Bridge.
  • Reds bar-betJohnny Bench could hold seven baseballs in one hand — and did it on national television to prove it.
