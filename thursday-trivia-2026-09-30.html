📅 This Day in History — September 30

  • 1955 — James Dean dies at 24. His Porsche 550 Spyder, nicknamed "Little Bastard," collided with a Ford at an intersection near Cholame, California. Only East of Eden had been released. Rebel Without a Cause and Giant came out after his death, and he became the first actor to receive a posthumous Oscar nomination (then got a second one for Giant). 71 years ago today.
  • 1927 — Babe Ruth hits No. 60. Ruth hit it off Washington's Tom Zachary at Yankee Stadium in the season's second-to-last game, and it stood as the single-season record for 34 years until Roger Maris hit 61 in 1961. Zachary argued for the rest of his life that the pitch was foul.
  • 1960 — The Flintstones premieres on ABC. It was the first animated series in prime time and was openly modeled on The Honeymooners. Its first sponsor was Winston cigarettes, and Fred and Barney lit up in the commercials.

Also today: Mozart's The Magic Flute premiered in Vienna in 1791, about ten weeks before he died; Boston dentist William Morton pulled a tooth under ether in 1846; FDR dedicated Hoover Dam in 1935; James Meredith was escorted onto the Ole Miss campus in 1962, setting off a night of rioting; Roberto Clemente doubled for his 3,000th hit in 1972 (it was his last regular-season hit; he died that Dec. 31); and Cheers premiered on NBC in 1982.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Sierra Leone? — Freetown (founded in 1792 as a settlement for formerly enslaved people, including Black Loyalists who had fought for Britain in the American Revolution and been resettled in Nova Scotia. It has one of the largest natural harbors in the world.)

What is the capital of Lesotho? — Maseru (Lesotho is completely surrounded by South Africa, and it is the only country whose entire territory lies above 1,000 meters (3,280 ft). Its lowest point is higher than the highest point of many countries.)

What is the capital of Samoa? — Apia (Robert Louis Stevenson spent his last years there and is buried on Mount Vaea above the town. In 2011 Samoa moved to the other side of the International Date Line and skipped Friday, Dec. 30 altogether.)

What is the capital of Luxembourg? — Luxembourg (City) (the capital of the world's last grand duchy. In 2020 Luxembourg became the first country to make all public transport free nationwide.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Commissioner Stewart McMillan on McMillan & Wife (1971–77)? — Rock Hudson (it ran in rotation on the NBC Mystery Movie alongside Columbo. Susan Saint James played his wife Sally, and Nancy Walker stole scenes as the housekeeper, Mildred.)

Who played Sgt. Pepper Anderson on Police Woman (1974–78)? — Angie Dickinson (it was the first successful hour-long U.S. drama with a woman in the lead, and police departments credited it with a rise in female applicants.)

Who played Jaime Sommers on The Bionic Woman (1976–78)? — Lindsay Wagner (Jaime was killed off on The Six Million Dollar Man, and viewers were upset enough that the network brought her back. Wagner won the 1977 Emmy for Lead Actress in a Drama.)

Who played Luke Spencer on General Hospital? — Anthony Geary (Luke and Laura's wedding in November 1981 drew about 30 million viewers, still the highest-rated hour in American soap history. Elizabeth Taylor made a guest appearance for it.)

Who played Dr. Michaela Quinn on Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman (1993–98)? — Jane Seymour (a Boston doctor sets up practice in 1860s Colorado Springs. Seymour was already known as the Bond girl Solitaire in Live and Let Die.)

Who played Officer Gunther Toody on Car 54, Where Are You? (1961–63)? — Joe E. Ross (his catchphrase was "Ooh! Ooh!" His partner Muldoon was Fred Gwynne, before The Munsters. The show came from Nat Hiken, who created Sgt. Bilko.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which novel begins "All children, except one, grow up"? — Peter Pan (Peter and Wendy, 1911) — J.M. Barrie (in 1929 Barrie gave the rights to London's Great Ormond Street Hospital for children, which still receives royalties.)

Who painted The Gleaners (1857), showing three peasant women bent over a harvested field? — Jean-François Millet (Paris critics saw it as a political provocation because it showed rural poverty at monumental scale. Van Gogh later copied Millet's work many times.)

Which composer wrote the William Tell Overture, best known as the Lone Ranger's theme? — Gioachino Rossini (1829. It was his last opera. He was 37 and lived almost 40 more years without writing another, devoting much of that time to cooking. Tournedos Rossini is named for him.)

Which 1888 poem ends "But there is no joy in Mudville — mighty Casey has struck out"? — "Casey at the Bat" — Ernest Lawrence Thayer (it first ran in the San Francisco Examiner. Thayer was paid about $5 and for years didn't even claim authorship.)

Who won the 1986 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction for Lonesome Dove? — Larry McMurtry (it began as an unmade 1970s screenplay meant for John Wayne, Jimmy Stewart and Henry Fonda. McMurtry also wrote The Last Picture Show and Terms of Endearment, and co-won an Oscar for Brokeback Mountain.)

Which literary heroine is forced to wear a scarlet "A," and who wrote her story? — Hester Prynne — Nathaniel Hawthorne, The Scarlet Letter (1850) (Hawthorne added the "w" to his surname partly to distance himself from ancestor John Hathorne, a judge at the Salem witch trials.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

Where and how was Pizza Hut founded? — Wichita, Kansas, 1958 (brothers Dan and Frank Carney, college students, borrowed $600 from their mother. The sign they bought only had room for about eight letters, so it became "Pizza Hut.")

How did Hewlett-Packard decide on its name? — A coin toss (Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard flipped for the order in 1939, working out of a Palo Alto garage now called the "birthplace of Silicon Valley." One of the first customers for their audio oscillator was Disney, for Fantasia.)

Who invented Lincoln Logs? — John Lloyd Wright, son of Frank Lloyd Wright (1916. He got the idea from the interlocking-timber foundations of his father's earthquake-resistant Imperial Hotel in Tokyo.)

What was the first toy ever advertised on television? — Mr. Potato Head (1952) (from Hasbro, based on George Lerner's idea. The first kits had only the face parts, and you pushed them into a real potato. The plastic body came in 1964.)

Who invented Kitty Litter, and how? — Edward Lowe, 1947 (a neighbor in Cassopolis, Michigan, whose sandbox had frozen asked him for some sand. He gave her absorbent clay from his family's business instead. It worked so well that he started bagging it.)

LocalWhich Cincinnati company sold the first refrigerator with shelves in the door? — Crosley — the "Shelvador" (Powel Crosley Jr.'s company introduced it in the 1930s. The same Crosley owned the Reds, radio giant WLW and later its own small car.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • AnimalsWood frogs freeze solid in winter. Up to about two-thirds of their body water turns to ice, and their hearts and breathing stop. Glucose protects their cells until the spring thaw, when they start moving again.
  • GeographyEverest isn't the point farthest from Earth's center. Because the planet bulges at the equator, the summit of Chimborazo in Ecuador is about 7,000 feet farther from the core, even though it is roughly 8,500 feet lower above sea level.
  • Language"Goodbye" is a contraction of "God be with ye." In the 1500s it was written "godbwye," and "God" later changed to "good" under the influence of "good day" and "good night."
  • SportsOnly one perfect game has ever been thrown in the postseason: Don Larsen's, for the Yankees in Game 5 of the 1956 World Series. The second postseason no-hitter didn't come until 2010, when Roy Halladay threw one against the Reds in the NLDS opener.
  • Pop CultureMister Rogers' cardigans were knitted by his mother, Nancy, who made him one every Christmas. One of them is in the Smithsonian.
  • HistoryThe Hundred Years' War lasted 116 years, from 1337 to 1453, with long truces in between. Nobody called it that at the time; historians named it in the 1800s.
  • SpaceSunsets on Mars are blue. Fine Martian dust scatters blue light forward, close to the Sun's direction, so the sky goes butterscotch by day and blue around the setting Sun. NASA rovers have photographed it.
  • BodyHumans appear to be the only animals that blush. Darwin called it "the most peculiar and the most human of all expressions."
  • FoodNachos were improvised in 1943 by Ignacio "Nacho" Anaya in Piedras Negras, Mexico, for a group of U.S. Army wives who showed up after the kitchen had closed. He used fried tortillas, cheese and jalapeños.
  • LocalThe Ohio River crested at 79.99 feet in Cincinnati on Jan. 26, 1937, the city's record flood. It's remembered for "Black Sunday," when floating gasoline caught fire in the Mill Creek valley.
