📅 This Day in History — October 2

  • 1950 — Peanuts debuts in just seven newspapers. Charles Schulz hated the name, which the syndicate chose. He wanted "Li'l Folks." He drew every strip himself for nearly 50 years. He died on Feb. 12, 2000, and his last Sunday strip ran the next day. 76 years ago today.
  • 1967 — Thurgood Marshall is sworn in as the first Black justice of the U.S. Supreme Court. As a lawyer he had won 29 of the 32 cases he argued before the Court, including Brown v. Board of Education (1954).
  • 1836 — Charles Darwin comes home. HMS Beagle reached Falmouth, England, after a voyage of almost five years, much longer than the planned two. He spent much of the trip seasick and published On the Origin of Species 23 years later. 190 years ago today.

Also today: Mahatma Gandhi born in 1869 (the U.N. marks the day as the International Day of Non-Violence); Alfred Hitchcock Presents premiered in 1955; The Twilight Zone premiered on CBS in 1959 with "Where Is Everybody?"; and President Woodrow Wilson suffered a disabling stroke in 1919, which his wife and doctor largely hid from the public.

🌍 Capitals Quiz

What is the capital of Australia? — Canberra (it was a compromise, because neither Sydney nor Melbourne would accept the other. Chicago architects Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin won the 1912 design competition, and Parliament moved there from Melbourne in 1927.)

What is the capital of Mauritania? — Nouakchott (it was a small fishing village built up into a capital at independence in 1960. Mauritania is also home to the Richat Structure, the 25-mile-wide "Eye of the Sahara" that astronauts use as a landmark.)

What is the capital of Barbados? — Bridgetown (it is Rihanna's hometown. In 2021 Barbados dropped the Queen and became a republic, and that same day Rihanna was named a National Hero. It is also the only foreign country George Washington ever visited, in 1751, and he caught smallpox there.)

What is the capital of Timor-Leste (East Timor)? — Dili (independent since May 20, 2002, it was the first new sovereign state of the 21st century. Its currency is the U.S. dollar.)

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Mr. French, the very proper butler, on Family Affair (1966–71)? — Sebastian Cabot (he looked after Buffy, Jody and Cissy for their uncle, played by Brian Keith. Buffy carried a doll named Mrs. Beasley.)

Who played bounty hunter Josh Randall, with his sawed-off "Mare's Leg" rifle, on Wanted: Dead or Alive (1958–61)? — Steve McQueen (this TV western made him a star before The Magnificent Seven and The Great Escape.)

Who played Sam McCloud, the New Mexico marshal who rode a horse through Manhattan? — Dennis Weaver (McCloud ran from 1970 to 1977. Earlier, Weaver had played limping deputy Chester on Gunsmoke, which won him an Emmy in 1959.)

Who played Officer Tom Hanson on 21 Jump Street (1987–91)? — Johnny Depp (it was one of the young Fox network's first hits. Depp hated being a teen idol and left after four seasons.)

Who played Vicki, the robot girl, on the syndicated sitcom Small Wonder (1985–89)? — Tiffany Brissette (V.I.C.I. stood for "Voice Input Child Identicant." She lived hidden in her inventor's family home and slept in a cabinet in her "brother's" room.)

Who played Superman on Lois & Clark: The New Adventures of Superman (1993–97)? — Dean Cain (Teri Hatcher played Lois. Cain had been a Princeton football star and signed with the Buffalo Bills before a knee injury ended his NFL plans.)

🎨 Arts & Literature

Which English novelist, born on this day in 1904, wrote Brighton Rock and the screenplay for The Third Man? — Graham Greene (he was considered for the Nobel Prize many times and never won.)

Which fictional detective got a front-page obituary in The New York Times in 1975? — Hercule Poirot (Agatha Christie) (it was published when Curtain, the book that kills him off, came out. Christie had written it in the 1940s and kept it locked away for decades.)

Which 1966 "nonfiction novel" is about the murder of a farm family in Holcomb, Kansas? — In Cold Blood — Truman Capote (his childhood friend from Monroeville, Alabama, Harper Lee, went with him to Kansas to help with the research.)

Which surrealist painted the melting clocks of The Persistence of Memory (1931)? — Salvador Dalí (the painting is only about 9½ × 13 inches. Dalí also designed the Chupa Chups lollipop logo in 1969.)

Which composer wrote the "Wedding March" that is traditionally played as newlyweds walk back up the aisle? — Felix Mendelssohn (it comes from his music for A Midsummer Night's Dream. "Here Comes the Bride," played on the way in, is Wagner's Bridal Chorus from Lohengrin.)

Which poet wrote "The fog comes / on little cat feet" and called Chicago "Hog Butcher for the World"? — Carl Sandburg (he won three Pulitzer Prizes: two for poetry and one for his biography of Abraham Lincoln.)

🏭 Founded & Invented

What machinery did Edward Dee repurpose to make the first Smarties candy in 1949? — Machines built to press gunpowder pellets (his company, Ce De Candy of New Jersey, now presses billions of rolls a year. Today is National Smarties Day.)

What did Nokia make when it was founded in Finland in 1865? — Paper (it was a pulp mill) (Fredrik Idestam built the mill on the Nokianvirta River, and the company later took the name of the nearby town of Nokia. It also made rubber boots and cables before phones.)

Where did the first Dairy Queen open in 1940? — Joliet, Illinois (Sherb Noble opened it to sell a soft-serve recipe created by the McCullough family. The upside-down Blizzard came along in 1985.)

Who founded Taco Bell in Downey, California, in 1962? — Glen Bell (he started out selling hot dogs and hamburgers at Bell's Drive-In, then switched to tacos after watching the lines at a Mexican restaurant across the street.)

What were the first words Thomas Edison recorded on his phonograph in 1877? — "Mary had a little lamb" (he recorded the nursery rhyme on tinfoil wrapped around a cylinder. Edison called the phonograph his favorite invention.)

LocalWhich Cincinnati toy company introduced the Easy-Bake Oven in 1963? — Kenner Products (the oven baked with two 100-watt light bulbs. Kenner, also from Cincinnati, later made the original Star Wars action figures in 1978.)

🧩 General Trivia

  • CarsThe first speeding ticket went to Walter Arnold of Kent, England, in 1896, for going 8 mph in a 2 mph zone. A policeman chased him down on a bicycle. Today is National Name Your Car Day.
  • CandyIn Canada and Britain, "Smarties" are chocolate: Nestlé's candy-coated buttons, similar to M&Ms. The American tablets are sold in Canada as "Rockets."
  • CandyM&M stands for Mars & Murrie: Forrest Mars and Bruce Murrie, son of a Hershey executive. The candy launched in 1941, and its first big customer was the U.S. military in WWII.
  • HistoryGandhi, born on this day in 1869, never won the Nobel Peace Prize. He was nominated five times, and the Nobel Committee later publicly regretted it.
  • FoodGerman chocolate cake isn't German. It's named for Sam German, an American who developed a sweet baking chocolate for Baker's in 1852.
  • FoodBuffalo wings were first served in 1964 at the Anchor Bar in Buffalo, N.Y., by Teressa Bellissimo. Chicken wings had been cheap scraps.
  • SportsThe football huddle was invented at Gallaudet, a university for deaf students, in the 1890s. Quarterback Paul Hubbard used it to keep opponents from reading his signed plays.
  • SportsSecretariat's 1973 Kentucky Derby time (1:59 2/5) is still the record, and he won the Belmont by 31 lengths. A necropsy estimated his heart at about 22 pounds, roughly 2½ times normal.
  • GeographySiberia's Lake Baikal holds more fresh water than all five Great Lakes combined, about a fifth of the world's unfrozen surface fresh water. It's over a mile deep.
  • GeographyThe smallest state used to have the longest official name: "State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations." Voters shortened it in 2020.
  • AnimalsAn ostrich's eye is bigger than its brain. At about 2 inches across, it's the largest eye of any land animal.
