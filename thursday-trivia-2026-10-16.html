📜 This Day in History — October 16

  • 1962 — The Cuban Missile Crisis begins. JFK is shown U-2 spy photos of Soviet missile sites in Cuba, starting the "Thirteen Days" that end on Oct 28.
  • 1968 — At the Mexico City Olympics, 200m gold and bronze medalists Tommie Smith and John Carlos raise black-gloved fists on the medal stand.
  • 1793 — Marie Antoinette is guillotined in Paris, nine months after her husband, Louis XVI.

🧠 General Trivia

  • HistoryThe 1834 Parliament fire started when workers burned piles of old wooden tally sticks, the Treasury's medieval accounting records, in furnaces under the House of Lords. J. M. W. Turner painted the blaze.
  • HistoryMarie Antoinette's reported last words were an apology to the executioner for stepping on his foot: "Pardon me, sir. I did not mean to do it."
  • OlympicsSilver medalist Peter Norman of Australia wore a human-rights badge on the 1968 podium in support of Smith and Carlos. When Norman died in 2006, Smith and Carlos were pallbearers at his funeral.
  • HistoryJohn Paul II was the first non-Italian pope in 455 years, since the Dutch Adrian VI in 1522.
  • BaseballThe Brewers began in 1969 as the Seattle Pilots, which lasted one season. Their 1982 pennant was in the American League; they moved to the NL in 1998. The Astros are the only other team with pennants in both leagues.
  • BaseballCleveland's last World Series title was in 1948, the longest current drought in MLB. The Rays have never won one; they lost in 2008 and 2020.
  • MoviesRaúl Julia's last theatrical film was 1994's Street Fighter, where he played M. Bison. It came out two months after he died.
  • WordsBefore his dictionary, Noah Webster wrote the "Blue-Backed Speller", which sold tens of millions of copies. He pushed American spellings like color and center.
  • AnimalsGlobal Cat Day: a group of kittens is called a kindle.
