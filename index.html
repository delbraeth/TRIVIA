📜 This Day in History — November 18

  • 1883 — The "Day of Two Noons." U.S. and Canadian railroads switched from dozens of local times to four standard time zones. Many towns saw noon twice as clocks were reset. Congress didn't make time zones law until 1918.
  • 1928 — Steamboat Willie premiered at the Colony Theatre in New York. It made Mickey Mouse a star and was one of the first cartoons with synchronized sound. Disney counts Nov 18 as Mickey's birthday.
  • 1963 — Bell introduced the Touch-Tone push-button phone, first offered in Carnegie and Greensburg, Pennsylvania. It had 10 buttons. The * and # keys came later.
  • Born today: Alan Shepard (1923), Margaret Atwood (1939), Linda Evans (1942), Kevin Nealon (1953), Elizabeth Perkins (1960), Owen Wilson (1968), Chloë Sevigny (1974).

🌍 Capitals

🇨🇦 Not Montreal: what's the capital of Quebec?
Quebec City. It's the only walled city in North America north of Mexico.

🇦🇺 What's the capital of the island state of Tasmania?
Hobart. It's Australia's second-oldest capital, founded in 1804.

🇩🇪 Which city, home of Oktoberfest, is the capital of Bavaria?
Munich

🇮🇹 What's the capital of Sicily, the largest island in the Mediterranean?

🎨 Arts & Literature

Disney's 1942 film was based on Bambi, a Life in the Woods (1923). Who wrote the novel?
Felix Salten, an Austrian writer

"Carlo Collodi" was the pen name of Carlo Lorenzini. Which wooden boy did he create in 1883?
Pinocchio. Collodi was the Tuscan village where his mother was born.

Which Australian-born author created Mary Poppins (1934) and famously disliked Disney's 1964 film?
P. L. Travers (Pamela Lyndon Travers, born Helen Lyndon Goff)

Which painter made The Starry Night (1889) from his room at the asylum in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence?
Vincent van Gogh. The painting hangs at MoMA in New York.

Beethoven set whose 1785 poem in the finale of his Ninth Symphony? Its melody is now the European anthem.
Friedrich Schiller, "Ode to Joy" ("An die Freude")

Who narrates The Great Gatsby? He's Daisy's cousin and Gatsby's neighbor on West Egg.
Nick Carraway

🏭 Founded & Invented

Today in 1963, the Bell System introduced which phone that replaced the rotary dial?
The Touch-Tone push-button phone, first offered in Carnegie and Greensburg, Pennsylvania

Which Canadian railway engineer proposed worldwide standard time zones in 1879, reportedly after missing a train in Ireland because of a misprinted timetable?
Sir Sandford Fleming

Frenchman André Cassagnes invented "L'Écran Magique." An Ohio toy company sold it from 1960 under what name?
Etch A Sketch, from the Ohio Art Company of Bryan, Ohio

Englishman William Addis started selling the first mass-produced bristle toothbrushes around 1780. Where did he come up with the idea?
In prison. He drilled holes in a bone saved from dinner and tied bristles into it.

Ben Cohen and Jerry Greenfield opened their first ice-cream shop in 1978 in a renovated gas station in which city?
Burlington, Vermont. They'd learned from a $5 correspondence course from Penn State.

In 1892, Dr. Washington Sheffield of New London, Connecticut, was the first to sell toothpaste in what?
A collapsible metal tube. Before that it came in jars.

🧩 General Trivia

  • Pop CultureMickey Mouse's first spoken words were "Hot dogs! Hot dogs!" in The Karnival Kid (1929).
  • FilmThe title Steamboat Willie spoofs Buster Keaton's Steamboat Bill, Jr., released earlier in 1928.
  • LawThe original Steamboat Willie Mickey entered the U.S. public domain on Jan 1, 2024, after 95 years.
  • Pop CultureWalt Disney first wanted to name his mouse Mortimer. His wife, Lillian, talked him into Mickey.
  • HistoryBefore standard time, every town set its own clock by the sun. Railroads were juggling dozens of local times, so noon in Chicago and noon in Pittsburgh were different moments.
  • TechA phone keypad puts 1-2-3 on the top row, but a calculator puts 7-8-9 on top. Bell's testing found the phone layout led to fewer dialing mistakes.
  • FoodVichyssoise, the cold potato-leek soup, has a French name but was created in New York, by chef Louis Diat at the Ritz-Carlton around 1917.
  • FoodJohnny Appleseed (John Chapman) planted apple nurseries across Ohio and Indiana. Most of his apples were for cider, not eating.
  • HolidayRiga, Latvia, claims the first decorated Christmas tree, in 1510. Latvia celebrates its 1918 independence proclamation today.
  • GeographyOman's Dhofar region grows frankincense. Its "Land of Frankincense" sites are on the UNESCO World Heritage list.
  • Football🦃 The Detroit Lions have hosted a Thanksgiving game every year since 1934, except during World War II (1939–44).
