📜 This Day in History — October 21

  • 1805 — Battle of Trafalgar. Nelson's fleet of 27 ships defeated 33 French and Spanish ships off Cape Trafalgar, Spain, without losing one. A sniper on the Redoutable killed Nelson aboard HMS Victory. London's Trafalgar Square and Nelson's Column honor the battle.
  • 1879 — Thomas Edison's lamp, with a carbonized cotton-thread filament, began burning at Menlo Park and lasted about 13½ hours. Oct 21 is the date usually given for the first practical incandescent bulb.
  • 1959 — The Guggenheim Museum opened on Fifth Avenue. Frank Lloyd Wright designed its spiral ramp but died six months before the opening.
  • Also: the Aberfan disaster in Wales (1966), when a colliery spoil tip slid onto a village school and killed 144 people, 116 of them children. And on Oct 21, 2015, Marty McFly arrived in the "future" of Back to the Future Part II.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceMars's moons Phobos and Deimos are named for Greek words for "fear" and "dread," the sons of Ares. Asaph Hall discovered both at the U.S. Naval Observatory in 1877.
  • SpacePhobos goes around Mars about three times a day and is slowly spiraling inward. In tens of millions of years it will break apart or crash into the planet.
  • MoviesBack to the Future Part II predicted the Cubs would win the 2015 World Series. In real life the Mets swept them in the NLCS that October, and the Cubs won the Series a year later.
  • MoviesEric Stoltz was originally cast as Marty McFly. Michael J. Fox replaced him after about six weeks of filming.
  • AnimalsFor Hagfish Day: a hagfish can turn a bucket of water into slime in seconds. It escapes its own goo by tying itself in a knot and sliding through.
  • AnimalsThe Komodo dragon is the largest living lizard. It can grow to about 10 feet and more than 150 pounds.
  • AnimalsNew Zealand's tuatara looks like a lizard but isn't one. It's the last survivor of a reptile order that lived alongside the dinosaurs, and it has a light-sensing "third eye" on top of its head.
  • ScienceIodine was discovered in 1811 by Bernard Courtois. He found it in seaweed ash while making saltpeter for Napoleon's gunpowder.
  • Did You KnowA standard barrel of oil is 42 U.S. gallons. The size was fixed by Pennsylvania producers in the 1860s and is still used today.
  • HistoryNelson's body was shipped home from Trafalgar preserved in a cask of brandy. That gave rise to the legend that sailors secretly drank from it ("tapping the admiral").
