📜 This Day in History — November 6

  • 1860 — Abraham Lincoln was elected the 16th president with about 40% of the popular vote. He wasn't even on the ballot in 10 Southern states. South Carolina seceded six weeks later.
  • 1869 — In New Brunswick, N.J., Rutgers beat Princeton 6–4 in what is considered the first college football game. Each side had 25 players, and the rules looked a lot more like soccer.
  • 1947 — Meet the Press debuted on NBC. It is the longest-running show in television history.
  • 1971 — The U.S. set off Cannikin, a roughly 5-megaton bomb, under Alaska's Amchitka Island. It was the largest American underground nuclear test ever.
  • Also: Tchaikovsky died in St. Petersburg (1893). Tammy Baldwin became the first openly gay person elected to the U.S. Senate (2012). Born today: Suleiman the Magnificent (1494), Adolphe Sax (1814), John Philip Sousa (1854), James Naismith (1861), James Jones (1921), Mike Nichols (1931), Sally Field (1946), Glenn Frey (1948), Maria Shriver (1955), Lori Singer (1957), Jerry Yang (1968), Ethan Hawke (1970), Rebecca Romijn and Thandiwe Newton (1972), Emma Stone (1988).

🌍 Capitals

🎩 Lincoln was elected on this date in 1860. What is the capital of his home state, Illinois? (Not Chicago.)
Springfield (Lincoln's home and tomb are there)

🏀 James Naismith invented basketball in Springfield, Massachusetts. Is Springfield the capital of Massachusetts? If not, what is?
No. It's Boston.

🏈 Rutgers and Princeton played the first college football game on this date in 1869. What is the capital of New Jersey? (Not Newark.)
Trenton (Washington crossed the Delaware to attack it in 1776)

💥 The Cannikin nuclear test was set off under Amchitka Island on this date in 1971. What is the capital of Alaska?
Juneau (no roads connect it to the rest of North America; you can only get there by air or sea)

🧩 General Trivia

  • FootballRutgers calls itself the "Birthplace of College Football." Princeton won the rematch a week later, 8–0.
  • MusicThe saxophone is made of brass, but it's a woodwind, because the sound comes from a reed, like a clarinet.
  • TVMeet the Press first aired on radio in 1945, two years before it moved to TV. Tim Russert hosted it from 1991 to 2008.
  • HollywoodEmma Stone's real first name is Emily. Another actress was already registered with the Screen Actors Guild as Emily Stone.
  • OscarsSally Field's 1985 Oscar speech for Places in the Heart is famously misquoted. She said, "You like me, right now, you like me!" — not "You really like me."
  • MusicThe Eagles' Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975), with Glenn Frey on lead vocals on several tracks, is the best-selling album in U.S. history. It's certified 38× Platinum, ahead of Thriller.
  • MusicTchaikovsky conducted on opening night at Carnegie Hall in 1891, two years before he died.
  • PoliticsLincoln was the first Republican president. He won with less than 40% of the popular vote in a four-way race.
  • NFLThe Bengals and Falcons will play in the Santiago Bernabéu, Real Madrid's stadium, which seats about 80,000 and has a retractable roof.
  • WordsThe OED's most famous outside contributor, W.C. Minor, was an American Civil War surgeon. He was held in England's Broadmoor asylum after killing a man in London in 1872.
