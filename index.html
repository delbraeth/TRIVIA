📅 This Day in History — September 22

  • 1862 — Lincoln issues the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, five days after Antietam, giving the Confederate states 100 days to return to the Union or see their slaves declared free on January 1. He had been sitting on the draft since July, waiting for a battlefield win so it wouldn't read as desperation.
  • 1776 — Nathan Hale, 21, is hanged by the British in Manhattan as a spy. The famous last line — "I only regret that I have but one life to lose for my country" — is a paraphrase of Joseph Addison's play Cato, which Hale knew well. 250 years ago today.
  • 1994 — Friends premieres on NBC. The pilot was titled "The One Where Monica Gets a Roommate," and the working title for the series had been Insomnia Cafe. All six leads were paid $22,500 per episode that first season.
  • 1927 — The "Long Count" fight: Gene Tunney beats Jack Dempsey at Soldier Field before 104,000 people after Dempsey's failure to go to a neutral corner delayed the count by roughly five seconds.

📍 Capitals Quiz

Capital of Slovenia? Ljubljana — its symbol is a dragon, and four of them guard the Dragon Bridge; local legend credits Jason and the Argonauts with founding the place.

Capital of Namibia? Windhoek — sits at about 5,600 feet in the central highlands; the name means "windy corner" in Afrikaans.

Capital of Nepal? Kathmandu — named for Kasthamandap, a wooden pavilion said to have been built from a single tree.

Capital of Papua New Guinea? Port Moresby — one of the few national capitals with no road connection to any other major town in its own country; you fly or you sail.

📺 Classic TV — Actors & Roles

Who played Alice the housekeeper on The Brady Bunch (1969–74)? Ann B. Davis — she'd already won two Emmys for The Bob Cummings Show before she ever picked up a Brady casserole.

Who played Wilbur Post, the only man Mister Ed would talk to, on Mister Ed (1961–66)? Alan Young — he later voiced Scrooge McDuck for Disney for more than 30 years.

Who played Sister Bertrille on The Flying Nun (1967–70)? Sally Field — she took the part reluctantly right after Gidget, and spent the next decade clawing her way out of it.

Who played reporter Carl Kolchak on Kolchak: The Night Stalker (1974–75)? Darren McGavin — the show lasted 20 episodes and became the admitted template for The X-Files.

Who played Fran Fine on The Nanny (1993–99)? Fran Drescher — she pitched the series to a CBS executive on a transatlantic flight.

🎨 Arts & Literature

Who wrote Middlemarch (1871)? George Eliot — pen name of Mary Ann Evans, who used it so her work wouldn't be filed under "lady novelist."

Who wrote the ghost story The Turn of the Screw (1898)? Henry James — 128 years on, readers still can't agree whether the ghosts are real or the governess is unraveling.

"Tyger Tyger, burning bright / In the forests of the night" — which poet? William Blake — from Songs of Experience (1794); he engraved and hand-colored every page himself.

Which Post-Impressionist painted The Card Players and obsessively returned to Mont Sainte-Victoire? Paul Cézanne — Picasso and Matisse both called him "the father of us all."

Who composed the Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2? Franz Liszt — the piece has been the soundtrack to more cartoon piano-recital disasters than any other in history.

Which novel won the 2007 Pulitzer for Fiction — a father and son pushing a shopping cart across a dead America? The Road, by Cormac McCarthy — he typed it on a portable Olivetti he'd used since 1963 and later auctioned for $254,500.

🏭 Founded & Invented

Who invented dynamite, and when? Alfred Nobel, 1867 — a premature obituary calling him "the merchant of death" is widely credited with prompting him to fund the Nobel Prizes.

The safety pin was patented in 1849 by Walter Hunt — how long did he spend inventing it, and what did he get? About three hours; he sold the patent for $400 — roughly $16,000 today, to settle a $15 debt.

When was Pixar founded as an independent company, and by whom? 1986 — Steve Jobs bought Lucasfilm's computer graphics division for $5 million and put in $5 million more. Disney bought it in 2006 for $7.4 billion.

Adidas and Puma were founded by two brothers who stopped speaking. Name them. Adolf "Adi" and Rudolf Dassler — one shoe company split into two in 1948–49 in the same German town, Herzogenaurach, which spent decades divided by which side of the river you worked on.

Where were Doritos invented? Disneyland, 1964 — a Frito-Lay executive saw a restaurant at the park frying up its stale tortillas rather than tossing them, and took the idea national.

LocalWhen was United Dairy Farmers founded? 1938, by Carl Lindner Sr. in Norwood — it started as a single drive-through dairy store; the Homemade Brand ice cream came later and never left.

🧩 General Trivia

  • SpaceA day on Venus is longer than its year. Venus takes 243 Earth days to turn once on its axis but only 225 to orbit the Sun — and it spins backwards, so the Sun there rises in the west.
  • BodyYour stomach lining replaces itself roughly every three to five days. It has to: otherwise it would digest itself.
  • GeographyRussia borders 14 countries — tied with China for the most of any nation. Russia also spans 11 time zones.
  • FoodWorcestershire sauce contains anchovies. The original 1837 batch was considered undrinkable, shoved in a barrel in a cellar, and rediscovered 18 months later — the aging had fixed it.
  • LanguageThe word "quarantine" comes from quaranta giorni — the 40 days Venetian authorities made ships wait offshore during the plague years.
  • AnimalsA group of flamingos is a "flamboyance." They're born gray; the pink comes from carotenoids in the brine shrimp and algae they eat.
  • ScienceGlass is not a slow-moving liquid. The old cathedral-window story is a myth — medieval glass was uneven when it was made. Glass is an amorphous solid.
  • HistoryThe Eiffel Tower was meant to be temporary — a 20-year permit for the 1889 World's Fair. It survived because the army found it useful as a radio antenna.
  • SportsA regulation golf ball has 300 to 500 dimples. Smooth balls fly about half as far; the dimples cut drag by controlling the turbulent boundary layer.
  • Pop CultureThe "Wilhelm scream" — the same stock scream recorded in 1951 — has been dropped into more than 400 films, including every original Star Wars and Indiana Jones picture.
  • Did You KnowHoney never spoils. Archaeologists have found 3,000-year-old Egyptian honey still edible — low moisture, high acidity, and hydrogen peroxide produced by an enzyme the bees add.
  • LocalCincinnati's Union Terminal (1933) has one of the largest half-domes in the Western Hemisphere — 180 feet wide, 106 feet high — and its rotunda is the acknowledged visual model for the Hall of Justice in Super Friends.
